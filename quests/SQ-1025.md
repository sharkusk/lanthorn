--- note 2026-08-23T19:06:05Z ---

## Measured on the new Shogun capture — our 8x16 native cell is RIGHT for Shogun, and the reason is worth knowing

`machine-screenshots/amiga-shogun-game.png` (720x570, 2x, overscan border). Every
pixel row is duplicated, which is what fixes the scale at exactly 2x.

VERTICAL: text lines repeat with a period of 16 device px = **8 rows in the
machine's 320x200 frame**. Topaz-8.

HORIZONTAL: three prose lines of different content, ink span divided by character
count, in the machine frame:

    "The gale tears at you,"          22 chars   86.0 px   3.91 px/char
    "biting deep within, and you"     27 chars  108.0 px   4.00 px/char
    "know that if you don't make"     27 chars  107.5 px   3.98 px/char

Flat at **4.00** — fixed pitch, and four is half of topaz's eight.

So Shogun's cell is 4x8 in the machine's 320x200 art frame, which is 8x16 in our
640x400 native space. That is exactly what we declare. Nothing to fix for Shogun.

## But the reason it comes out right is NOT that we double an 8x8 font

Topaz is 8 pixels wide. Measuring 4 art pixels per character means the text is
drawn in the **hires 640-wide space**, where a pixel is half as wide as an art
pixel — `gfx.c`'s design, art in fat units and text in thin ones, one address
space. One text pixel is half an art pixel wide and a whole art pixel tall, so an
8x8 face lands on 8x16 native. The two errors cancel exactly, which is why nothing
has ever looked wrong here.

## And Arthur does NOT do this — the space is per-game

Arthur's `char.data` averages 5.21 px of advance from its own `tf_CharSpace`
table, and `amiga-arthur-text.png` measures its prose at 4.70 art px/char (prose
skews narrow against the whole-set mean). Those agree in the **320 art space**. In
the hires space the same face would average 2.6 art px/char, which is half what
the capture shows.

So Arthur draws text in the fat space and Shogun in the thin one. That is
`extern int Hirez;` in `gfx.c` — declared in the archived tree and defined nowhere
in it, which is the strongest evidence yet that it is a per-game flag rather than
a machine constant, and it means **"the Amiga's Version 6 cell" is not one number**.

## Where this leaves the report

Nothing found fat yet. Shogun measures correct, and Arthur is wrong in the other
direction (too NARROW and too SHORT: it wants ~9.4 native advance and a 20-row
line where we give 8 and 16), which is SQ-1009's subject. Needs the press and the
frame where the fat rendering was seen before there is anything to fix — a font-3
run, a Macintosh press and a raster prose line are three different paths through
`blit_glyph_styled` and only one of them can be the one.

## Resolved — no defect. Withdrawn by the reporter, and the measurement agrees.

The reporter revised it to "a difference between our font and the default Amiga",
which is exactly what the numbers above say. Shogun's machine cell measures 4x8 in
the 320x200 art frame = 8x16 native, and 8x16 native is what we declare and draw.
The pitch is right; `vga16` simply does not look like topaz at that pitch.

Nothing to change. What survives is the reason it comes out right, which was not
understood before and is recorded above: Shogun's text lives in the **hires
640-wide space** where a pixel is half an art pixel wide, so topaz's 8 columns land
on 4 art columns, and its 8 rows land on 8 art rows. Half the width and all of the
height — the two cancel to our 8x16 native cell.

And the finding that outlives the report: **Arthur does not do this.** Its face
measures 4.70 art px/char against its own table's 5.21, which only works in the
320 art space; in the hires space it would be 2.6. So the text space is per-game —
`extern int Hirez;`, declared in the archived `gfx.c` and defined nowhere in it —
and "the Amiga's Version 6 cell" is not one number. That belongs to SQ-1009.
