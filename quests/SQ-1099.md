## The measurement

`resize_directional` uses **Nearest** when growing, deliberately, because at a whole
factor it is exact: it duplicates rows and columns and never blends. At a fractional
factor it duplicates SOME and not others, so one frame carries two stroke widths.
Measured over a 640-wide press:

| s | composite | art-pixel widths |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 640px | 1px x640 — uniform |
| 1.25 | 800px | 1px x480, 2px x160 |
| **1.5** | 960px | **1px x320, 2px x320** |
| 1.9 | 1216px | 1px x64, 2px x576 |
| 2.0 | 1280px | 2px x640 — uniform |
| 2.5 | 1600px | 2px x320, 3px x320 |
| 3.0 | 1920px | 3px x640 — uniform |

At 1.5x **half the strokes in the frame are one device pixel and half are two**. That
is the "every edge is interpolated" the original report described — nothing is
actually blended, but a 2:1 stroke variation across one drawing reads as softness, and
on Infocom pixel art (one-pixel rules, dithered shading, bitmap type) it is exactly
the wrong artefact.

SQ-1081 shipped a fix for a DIFFERENT mechanism in the same area — the terminal
re-stretching the composite by ~1.3% when it missed the cell grid. That is real, it is
confirmed, and it is a rounding error beside this. Read SQ-1081 before starting here;
its notes carry the distinction and the cell table.

## The fix, and the property that makes it safe

A **box / area magnification** instead of Nearest. The reason to think this is
tractable rather than a fight:

> a box magnification is **identical to Nearest at whole factors**

So it cannot disturb 1x, 2x, 3x or any rung the corpus is tested at — the frames it
changes are exactly the frames that are currently wrong. That matters because two
existing tests pin the current behaviour as "not negotiable":
`resampling_tracks_the_single_resample_ideal_in_every_direction` and
`magnification_invents_no_colours`. Read both before assuming they must be relaxed —
the invariant they protect ("magnification is bit-exact pixel replication") holds
unchanged wherever they measure it, so the question is whether their WORDING outlaws
a change their assertions do not.

## Scope, and where it reaches

`resize_directional`'s magnifying arm is shared. A change there reaches every
magnifying site in the app — Glulx `draw_image`, inline pictures, cover art — not just
v6. Decide deliberately whether it lands there or in a v6-only path, and say why;
"it also improves cover art" may be true and is still a bigger blast radius than the
report asked for.

## Check against a reference before pinning what "right" looks like

`machine-screenshots/` holds captures on real hardware. Note the honest limit,
recorded on SQ-1094: no real machine ever scaled its own screen, so a capture gives
the artwork's intended texture, NOT what a magnified frame should look like. It can
tell you a stroke should read as uniform; it cannot tell you what 1.5x should do.
