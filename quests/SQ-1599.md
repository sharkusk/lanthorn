hybrid_chrome_layout / V6HybridChromeLayout (render/screen.rs ~5294) publishes each chrome run's starting cell, the text strip vs over-art split, the menu band rows and the story viewport. A host that draws the chrome itself still differs from the terminal renderer in five places, all decided at draw time in render/screen.rs:
1. Text strip ground and flood: each text strip's rect and the colour draw_chrome_text_strip floods it with (~9390-9425), and the full-width flood of bar rows. Without them, Arthur's status bar shows one-cell gaps between its fields.
2. Rule stretch (SQ-0747): rules stretched to their neighbours at draw time. Without it, Journey Amiga's collapsed rules stop a cell or two short of a heading or corner, most visibly on wide panes.
3. Flank border glyphs: the side rules and picture divider drawn in cells (flank_borders / divider_exts, ~3824-3825). Without them, on wide panes the flank rules meet the band's cell rules a few pixels off.
4. Story-slot grid: grids drawn by render_node inside the story slot, such as Shogun's boot menu ('START the game'), aren't in the layout.
5. Settled cells at narrow widths: at about 39 columns the published layout overlaps words with dividers, arrows and rules (Journey's menus), and at small scale puts two of Shogun's title rows in one cell row. The terminal settles each cell at draw time.
Also useful: the scale of the picture ring/strip for plans other than Menu (the click map exposes only the menu band's scale), so a host can place the picture without assuming top anchoring.
Wanted: publish these in the layout (strip rects plus ground colours, stretched rule extents, the flank and divider cells, story-slot grid runs, and final settled cells) so a host draws exactly what the terminal draws. Test stories: Arthur, Journey (PC r83 and Amiga), Shogun, Zork Zero, at wide and narrow panes.
