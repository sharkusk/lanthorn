## Settled: each backend uses the best emphasis it has

**User's decision (2026-08-24):** hybrid keeps the terminal's italic; raster underlines. The two backends deliberately differ on the same frame.

The reasoning is that the two have different faces available. On a cell path the face is the player's own terminal font and `Modifier::ITALIC` (SGR 3) is exactly the request "draw this with your italic face" — lanthorn synthesises nothing and the result is a real italic. In raster lanthorn holds a release's own bitmap face, no shipped face carries an italic variant, and a synthesised shear is not something any machine drew.

So no further change is needed. `5a465951` landed the raster half — `zvm::interpreter::V6Emphasis`, Amiga and Macintosh `Underline`, every other row keeping the slope for want of a capture — and hybrid is correct untouched.

**A rule that was proposed and rejected:** that emphasis should be italic wherever a real italic FACE is available, keyed on the face rather than the machine. The captures contradict it. `machine-screenshots/mac-shogun.jpg` rules under `Erasmus`, and the Macintosh had real italics — it simply did not use them for this bit. Face availability does not explain what the machines did; the machines underlined.

## A correction to this quest's own premise, worth keeping

The word the captures rule under — `Erasmus` in "This is the bridge of the Erasmus" — is PROSE, and hybrid draws prose through `transcript::draw_str_runs` → `render::apply_text_style`, never through `screen::v6_run_style`. Measured on `James Clavell's Shogun.adf` (release 295 / serial 890321), two inputs past the boot menu: no `PxText` on that frame carries §8.7.1's Italic bit and the transcript run does. Anyone chasing a v6 prose STYLE defect should start at `apply_text_style`; `v6_run_style` serves painted runs — status bars, menu strips, chrome.

`v6_shogun_emphasis_rule` now pins the frame as a fixture and the half of the rule that holds either way: the bit arrives, as exactly one rendering, on the word and not the line.
