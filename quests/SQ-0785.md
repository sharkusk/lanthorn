THE USER'S ASK: integrate this or an equivalent, gated by config, **defaulted ON** to help stabilise the map.

WHAT MUST BE MEASURED BEFORE ADOPTING, because the design's whole risk is here:
1. **Cost per move.** It snapshots the live game into a second session and runs a command in it, after EVERY directional move. Journey and the Glulx titles are the expensive cases; Counterfeit Monkey's init was 23.78M opcodes before SQ-0378's acceleration. An on-by-default feature that adds a silent turn to every move needs a measured per-move cost on the slowest engine, not an estimate.
2. **Which thread.** The standing direction is that only the story interpreter runs on the main thread and map work moves off it. A probe that snapshots and steps a second session on the main thread walks straight into that; the natural home is the mapper worker.
3. **Engine neutrality.** "Snapshot the live game" is the host-snapshot family (Save State), which must behave uniformly across zvm/gvm/scott. Verify the probe works on all three rather than only on the Z-machine, or gate it per-engine and say so.
4. **Side effects the snapshot cannot undo.** A silent probe turn advances the game's own clocks, daemons, and RNG inside the disposable copy — fine — but it must not touch anything outside it: no transcript, no sound, no autosave, no screen. The commit claims silence; that is the thing to falsify first.
5. **Correctness of the claim.** "Closing one-way map gaps without inventing reciprocity" is the valuable part and the part to test hardest: a probe that guesses wrong writes a WRONG edge into the map, which is worse than a missing one. Zork 1's maze and any title with asymmetric or conditional exits are the oracles.
