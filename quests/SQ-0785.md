THE USER'S ASK: integrate this or an equivalent, gated by config, **defaulted ON** to help stabilise the map.

WHAT MUST BE MEASURED BEFORE ADOPTING, because the design's whole risk is here:
1. **Cost per move.** It snapshots the live game into a second session and runs a command in it, after EVERY directional move. Journey and the Glulx titles are the expensive cases; Counterfeit Monkey's init was 23.78M opcodes before SQ-0378's acceleration. An on-by-default feature that adds a silent turn to every move needs a measured per-move cost on the slowest engine, not an estimate.
2. **Which thread.** The standing direction is that only the story interpreter runs on the main thread and map work moves off it. A probe that snapshots and steps a second session on the main thread walks straight into that; the natural home is the mapper worker.
3. **Engine neutrality.** "Snapshot the live game" is the host-snapshot family (Save State), which must behave uniformly across zvm/gvm/scott. Verify the probe works on all three rather than only on the Z-machine, or gate it per-engine and say so.
4. **Side effects the snapshot cannot undo.** A silent probe turn advances the game's own clocks, daemons, and RNG inside the disposable copy — fine — but it must not touch anything outside it: no transcript, no sound, no autosave, no screen. The commit claims silence; that is the thing to falsify first.
5. **Correctness of the claim.** "Closing one-way map gaps without inventing reciprocity" is the valuable part and the part to test hardest: a probe that guesses wrong writes a WRONG edge into the map, which is worse than a missing one. Zork 1's maze and any title with asymmetric or conditional exits are the oracles.

RELATED: the mapper's layout/routing work, and the background-processing direction ([[background-processing-direction]] — the goal of moving graph ownership to a worker).

## Two decisions after the first pass (user, 2026-08-28)

The lane reported — correctly, rather than resolving it — that "always visible"
and "lives on the map pane's border" conflict: `draw_map_panel` is only reached
in the split layout, so with `show_map` false there is no pane and the control
vanishes.

**1. The feature is NOT disabled when the map is hidden.**

Hiding a view must not degrade the data behind it. The mapper keeps recording
rooms and edges with the pane closed, because it is driven by turns rather than
by rendering; a player who plays with the map hidden and opens it later would
otherwise find gaps they could never account for. And the feature is **opt-in and
default off** — someone who switched it on asked for a better map, and switching
it off because they collapsed a pane second-guesses an explicit choice.

The cost objection is weaker than it looks: the gate means a probe runs only
where no return path is known, and the persisted `probed` record means a room is
searched once per **map**, not per visit. The waste is bounded and decays.

**2. The control moves off the map pane, to sit BESIDE the map toggle** on the
story pane's bottom border, at the right where the map toggle already is.

That follows from (1) — a feature that runs with the map hidden cannot have its
only switch on the map pane, or you could not turn off something that is running.
It also groups the two map-related controls together: the toggle for the pane,
and the toggle for the thing that improves what the pane shows.

The user's observation that settles the width worry: **the title strip is on the
TOP border, so the bottom row has the full width to itself.** SQ-1123's give-way
threshold was measured for a centred *pair* with a title competing above; the
bottom row is under no such pressure.

Resulting bottom border: **band + guidance centred** (plus SQ-1107's flashlight
when it lands), **map + return probe at the right**.

**Not yet implemented** — folded into SQ-1107's lane, which is already editing
the same border layout. Two lanes editing that code concurrently would collide.
