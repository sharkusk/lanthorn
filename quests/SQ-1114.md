--- note 2026-08-28T05:05:28Z ---

## Why these four stand out

The SQ-1110 harvest read 119 stories out of 353 files and accounted for every one
of the 70 skips as a **correct refusal**: InvisiClues `*izm.z5`/`*inv.z5` carry no
grammar, `.blb` files that are resource-only have no executable, `Grammar:
Absent` is the menu-driven v6 titles, and disk images are not read at all
(mounting lives in `app`, and every floppy game in the corpus also ships bare).

These four are the exception — they **load an executable** and then yield **zero
verbs**:

- `advent.blb`
- `narco.blorb`
- `sensory.blorb`
- `photo201.blb`

`advent.blb` is the tell. Adventure has verbs; a reading that finds none is
almost certainly wrong rather than a story that genuinely has no grammar. The
lane did not chase it, correctly — it was out of that quest's scope — but it is
the one unexplained result in an otherwise fully-accounted harvest.

## Where to look

Most likely candidates, in order:

1. **The wrong chunk is being executed.** A Blorb may carry more than one
   executable resource; check which chunk the harvester picked and whether it is
   the story or something else.
2. **The engine dispatch.** If these are Glulx, `gvm::grammar` derives the table
   address by closing a dictionary → actions → grammar chain and **refuses** when
   it cannot close it (`TablesNotFound`). A refusal is not zero verbs, so
   distinguish the two — the harvester may be flattening an error into an empty
   result.
3. **Not Inform output.** SQ-1101 established that two Z-machine stories in the
   corpus are not Inform 6 output at all and are correctly refused. If any of
   these four is in that class, zero verbs is the right answer and this quest
   closes as documented behaviour.

## First step

Run `verb-synonyms-gen`'s harvest against these four alone and read its stderr
report — it distinguishes refusals from empty results, which is exactly the
distinction needed here. Do not assume a defect before that separates them.

## Root cause: the verb-number base, not the executable and not the locator

All four are **Glulx**, and all four load, locate and read their tables perfectly.
The defect is one subtraction.

A Glulx dictionary record's `#dict_par2` does not hold a verb's grammar-table
index; it holds that index **subtracted from a base**, so verbs count DOWN
(`Inform6/text.c`: *"The verb number is inverted (we count down from $FF/$FFFF)
and stored in #dict_par2"*). `gvm::grammar` knew one base, `$FFFF`. Inform has
used two in Glulx mode:

| Inform | `verbs.c` |
|---|---|
| 6.21(G0.35) – **6.31** | `dictionary_add(English_verbs_given[i], …, 0xff-Inform_verb, 0);` |
| **6.32** onwards | `(glulx_mode)?(0xffff-Inform_verb):(0xff-Inform_verb), 0);` |

(Checked at the tags: `v6.30`/`v6.31` `verbs.c:691,740` against `v6.32`
`verbs.c:693,742`; on master the same choice lives in
`text.c::dictionary_set_verb_number` line 2502. Layout cross-checked against
Plotkin's *Glulx Inform Technical Reference* §"Dictionary Word Structure".)

Every Glulx story in the corpus is stamped 6.32–6.41 at bytes 44..48 of the
image — **except these four, which are all 6.21**. Read against `$FFFF`, all 225
of `advent.blb`'s verb-flagged records named verb 65,388 of a 109-verb table, so
the reader produced a full grammar table with **zero verb WORDS**, and the
harvester's `absorb` correctly reported "no verbs".

So of the quest's three hypotheses: (1) no — the right chunk was executed; (2) no
— no refusal was involved, `Grammar::load` returned `Ok` every time; (3) no —
they are unambiguously Inform 6 output.

## SQ-1101 is a DIFFERENT defect, not a neighbour of this one

| | SQ-1114 | SQ-1101 |
|---|---|---|
| engine | Glulx | Z-machine |
| compiler | Inform 6.21 (`"6.21"` at image+44) | not Inform at all (`1a01`, `0m03` at header `$3C`, where Inform stamps `6.NN`) |
| symptom | `Ok`, tables located, 0 verb words | `Err(BadVerbTable)`, no table located |
| stage | DECODING a located table | LOCATING a table that may not exist |

Confirmed on disk: `stories/frankenfingers_260330.z5` and
`stories/ImpossibleStairs.z8` still return `BadVerbTable` after this fix, and
their `$3C..$3F` bytes are `1a01`/`0m03` (compare `advent.z8`: `6.32`). Nothing
here touches SQ-1101, and SQ-1101's diagnosis is unaffected.

## The fix

`gvm::grammar` now decides the base per file, and by a CHECK rather than by
reading Inform's version string: the grammar table states how many verbs it
holds, so a base is right only if every verb-flagged dictionary record names one
of them. The two acceptance windows cannot overlap at any verb count the reader
accepts (`MAX_VERBS` is 20,000; the windows are 0xFF00 apart). A file where
neither passes keeps the modern base, so nothing that read before reads
differently.

## Result

All four now yield verbs, and every one of them names **every** verb in its
table:

| story | base | verbs | verb words | was |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `advent.blb` | `$FF` | 109 | 225 | 0 |
| `narco.blorb` | `$FF` | 103 | 216 | 0 |
| `sensory.blorb` | `$FF` | 105 | 212 | 0 |
| `photo201.blb` | `$FF` | 85 | 176 | 0 |

Adventure answers `xyzzy` and `plugh`; `take` = carry/hold/take, `go` =
go/run/walk. All 22 previously-working stories are byte-for-byte unchanged
(`$FFFF`). `harvest::sweep` over the four: `read=4 glulx=4 verbs=580 skipped=0`.

## Why the existing sweep could not see it

`every_glulx_story_either_reads_or_refuses` matched only `.gblorb`,
`.gblorb.blorb` and `.ulx` — and all four of these live in `.blb`/`.blorb`. Both
gaps are closed: the extension list now includes them (Z-machine blorbs fall out
at `glulx_image`), and the sweep asserts the invariant that actually failed —
**every word the dictionary flags a verb reaches one** (0 exceptions across 26
stories; 225/225 exceptions in Adventure before the fix). Deliberately NOT the
converse: six stories have verb entries with no word at all, which is ordinary
`Extend only … replace`.

## Falsified

Reverting `verb_number_base` to always answer `$FFFF`:
`every_glulx_story_either_reads_or_refuses` fails with *"sensory.blorb: abstract
is flagged a verb and reaches none"* — the reported symptom — and both new pins
fail with `left: 65535, right: 255`.
