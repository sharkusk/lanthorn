## Why this is the one worth building

Infocom's "walking dead" states — the game continues for hours after becoming
unwinnable — exist **because the hardware of the day could not check**. You cannot
fork a 1980s Z-machine. We can, cheaply, and we already do: host Save States are
engine-neutral snapshots and `session.rs` can run a second silent session.

That makes this the clearest case of spending 2026 compute on something period
hardware structurally could not do, rather than on decoration.

## The mechanism already exists, filed for another purpose

**SQ-0785** — "re-land the return probe: snapshot the live game into a silent
second session after each move to discover the way back" — is exactly this
machinery, currently aimed at automapping. Generalising it:

1. snapshot after each move
2. in a forked, silent session, try to get BACK — the inverse direction, or a
   short search
3. if nothing returns, the move was irreversible

Build the two on one probe rather than two. Two forked-session probes would be the
parallel-path shape CLAUDE.md's refactoring policy warns about, and the automap
half is already specified.

## Keep the warning COARSE, and opt-in

    lanthorn: that looked irreversible.

and nothing more. Not *why*, not *you needed the lamp*, not *you can no longer
win*. The moment it explains, it is a walkthrough. A coarse flag lets a player
decide to rewind; a specific one decides the puzzle for them.

Off by default. Some players want the 1982 experience exactly as it was, and a
game that warns you is a different game.

## Honest limits — state them rather than overselling

* **Irreversible is not the same as unwinnable.** Plenty of one-way moves are
  fine, and some fatal mistakes are perfectly reversible in the movement graph
  (smashing an object, using up a limited resource). This detects a
  ONE-WAY-ROOM-TRANSITION, which is a proxy and will both miss and false-positive.
  Say so in the UI wording, or it will be trusted past what it can support.
* **Cost.** A probe per move, per candidate direction. Measure it —
  `examples/scroll_bench` (SQ-1033) is the precedent for building the instrument
  before claiming a cost — and put the work off the main thread, which is the
  standing direction: only the story interpreter belongs there.
* **Determinism.** A forked session must not consume the RNG the live one will
  use, or the probe changes the game it is probing. `random_seed` is already
  threaded through `GameSession::new_for_machine`; make sure the fork is sealed.

Related: SQ-0785 (the probe), and the persistence rules in CLAUDE.md — a snapshot
carries the recipe, not the result.

## Scheduled: 0.4, and it is the LAST of the cluster

Tagged `theme=assist`, `release=0.4`. Sequenced last of the six because it is the
most expensive and the only one that needs machinery that does not exist yet.

**Confirmed at scheduling: one probe, not two.** SQ-0785 (the return probe, aimed
at automapping) is now tagged into the same theme, and the two are to be built on
a single fork-and-probe seam. Two forked-session probes would be exactly the
parallel-path shape CLAUDE.md's refactoring policy warns about — two callers
maintaining the same invariant across files, where the cure is a type. Whichever
of the two is implemented first owns the seam; the second consumes it.

Also gated on SQ-1045, like every other consumer in the cluster: the warning is
a line printed at the player, so its register is not this quest's to invent.
