## The idea

A **lanthorn** is a lantern — the archaic spelling, the horn-paned kind. And the
lamp is the single most iconic object in the genre; Zork's brass lantern is the
thing everyone remembers. So when a helper kicks in, it can read as the interpreter
holding up a light rather than as tool output:

    Lanthorn shines a helping light…

That is worth deciding ONCE and applying across every assist feature — SQ-1041's
vocabulary offer, SQ-1042's completion, SQ-1043's irreversibility warning, and
whatever follows — rather than each growing its own register.

## THE CONSTRAINT THAT SHAPES IT: never mistakable for the story

Infocom's prose has a strong voice, and its parser already speaks in brackets:

    [I don't know the word "illuminate".]

A helper writing in the same register, in the same stream, is genuinely confusing
— and worse than confusing when the helper is WRONG, because the player will
attribute our mistake to the game. Atmosphere must not cost attribution.

Two things keep it honest, and both already exist:

* **`TranscriptKind`.** `push_transcript_kind` already separates app-generated
  lines from `TranscriptKind::Story`, and the transcript filter can hide them. Use
  that rather than inventing a marker — it is what makes an assist line
  distinguishable to the CODE as well as to the eye.
* **`style.toml`.** Standing rule: every new UI element must be themeable via a
  selector (ColorScheme field + `style.rs` selector + render apply), never
  hard-coded. A player who wants the 1982 experience should be able to make these
  lines plain, or the filter should hide them entirely.

So: flavour in the WORDS, separation in the KIND and the STYLE. The line can be
warm without pretending to be the game.

## Some judgement about how far to take it

Once per event, not once per suggestion — a lantern that announces itself every
time a completion fires is a paperclip. Consider the flavour on the FIRST use in a
session and a quiet form thereafter, or attach it to the feature turning on rather
than to each firing.

And it should not be the only thing said. "Lanthorn shines a helping light" is
atmosphere; the player still needs the words the story actually knows. Flavour
leads, information follows.

Related: SQ-1041, SQ-1042, SQ-1043 (the features that will speak), and the
styleable-UI rule in CLAUDE.md.

## Refinement — the prefix IS the attribution, so it cannot taper away

The note above suggested showing the flourish on first use and a quiet form
afterwards, to stop it becoming a paperclip. That is half right and the wrong half
was load-bearing.

**The prepended statement is not decoration; it is the mechanism.** It marks the
line as OURS before any content arrives, which is what stops a player attributing
our suggestion — or our mistake — to the story. `TranscriptKind` and `style.toml`
carry that separation for the code and for the eye, but neither survives a
copy-paste, a transcript file (SQ-0410), a screen reader, or a screenshot in a bug
report. The words do.

So the two jobs split:

* **The MARKER is mandatory, on every assist line.** No exceptions, no tapering.
  It is how the line is attributable in every form the text can take.
* **The FLOURISH is what tapers.** Full on first use in a session, brief
  thereafter:

        Lanthorn shines a helping light — this story knows  light · turn on · burn
        Lanthorn: light · turn on · burn

Same identity both times, so attribution never weakens; only the prose does. That
also answers the paperclip worry without paying for it in clarity.

Corollary for anyone implementing an assist: **do not emit an unmarked line**, even
a short one, even a very obviously-ours one. The moment one slips through unmarked,
the convention stops being a guarantee and becomes a habit — and a reader can no
longer tell by looking.

## Scheduled: 0.4, and it goes FIRST

0.4's theme is this cluster — `theme=assist`, `release=0.4`: SQ-1045, SQ-1044,
SQ-1042, SQ-1040, SQ-1041, SQ-1043 (+ SQ-0785, which SQ-1043 shares a probe with).

Raised to `high` because this one is a **blocker rather than a feature**. It is a
decision, not code, and SQ-1041's vocabulary offer, SQ-1042's completion and
SQ-1043's irreversibility warning all print through whatever it settles. Deciding
it after two of them have shipped means retrofitting register across three
features and re-testing every string — so it lands before any of them, even
though on its own it is the smallest item in the set.

What "landing" means here is narrow, and worth saying so the quest cannot expand:
the register, the prefix, the style selector and the rule that keeps an assist
line unmistakable for the story's own voice — plus one worked example per
consumer. Not the consumers themselves.
