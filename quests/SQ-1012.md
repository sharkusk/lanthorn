## What was seen

On the Macintosh **colour** press, with `set-v6-pixel-lock` enabled, more of each
descender disappears than without it. Reported by eye against the 7x15 cell
SQ-0917 introduced.

## Why this is probably NOT the same bug as the face fallback

Two descender defects were in play the same afternoon and they must not be
conflated:

1. **The face fallback** (fixed in SQ-0917's follow-up): `blit_glyph_styled` chose
   between the 8x16 face and the 8x8 master on `ch >= 16`, so a 15-tall cell fell
   through to the 8x8 chain. Measured at 15 tall, `y` bottoms 3 rows below `x`
   with the proper face and only **1** with the fallback — the tail is
   substantially there in both, just far shallower in one.
2. **This**, which is an ADDITIONAL loss on top, and only with pixel lock, and
   only reported on the colour press.

The blit happens at the NATIVE cell and knows nothing about pixel lock, so a
defect that only appears when the lock is on is almost certainly downstream of the
glyph — in how the native canvas is then scaled to the pane. A descender is the
bottom one or two scanlines of a glyph, which is exactly what a resample drops
first when it lands on an unlucky ratio.

## Where to look

- `v6_pixel_lock` and the locked scale path (SQ-0940, SQ-0978). The lock changes
  the scale to an integer ratio; work out what ratio the Mac COLOUR press lands on
  and whether the bottom rows of each 15-tall cell survive it.
- The colour press is 640x400 and the mono 480x300, so they lock to different
  ratios against the same pane — which would explain why only one was reported.
  **Check the mono press too before assuming it is colour-only.**
- Compare against the same frame with the lock off, at the same pane size, so the
  only variable is the lock.

## Do not start until

SQ-0917's render path is actually complete. As of filing, `render/screen.rs` still
carries **35 production sites** that hardcode 8 and 16 as bare literals rather
than reading the session cell, so Macintosh geometry is half-applied and any
measurement taken now is of a screen with two disagreeing cell sizes in it. That
work has to land first or this quest will chase a symptom of it.

## DIAGNOSED — and it is not a descender defect (2026-08-22)

The original report framed this as descenders losing more of their tail under the
lock. That framing is wrong and should be set aside. The real defect is broader
and has a precise cause.

### The mechanism

The pixel lock exists to make one **ART** pixel occupy a whole number of **device**
pixels. On a press whose artwork is PREDOUBLED — the Macintosh colour archive is
320x200 art scaled 2x onto a 640x400 native screen — one art pixel is already two
native pixels. So a rung that is whole in art terms is a **HALF-STEP** in native
terms.

`graphics.rs`'s own test says so outright:

> "`art_scale` (2, 2) puts the rungs on half-steps, so the lock quantizes down to
> 2.5x — and 2.5 unit pixels per art pixel of 2 unit pixels is 5 whole device
> pixels, which is the entire point of the lock."
> — `the_pixel_lock_ladder_still_governs_when_the_cap_is_gone`

**Text is drawn at the NATIVE cell**, not in art space. So a native scale of 2.5
turns a 7-pixel-wide glyph into 17.5 device pixels: its columns alternate two and
three pixels wide, and its 15 rows likewise. The letterforms come apart.

### Why the black-and-white press is always fine

`Pic.data` is 480x300 at `art_scale` **(1, 1)** — art pixel IS native pixel — so
every rung is a whole native step and text scales cleanly at any of them. The
user reported exactly this: "the b/w version always looks good". That observation
is what identified the mechanism, and it is the discriminator to test against.

### This is PRE-EXISTING, not caused by SQ-1011

The half-step ladder predates the Macintosh font by some months. What SQ-1011
changed is visibility: `FONT` 524 is a crisper, purpose-drawn face, so its
letterforms break down more legibly than `vga16`'s did. The cell change (SQ-0917)
raised it too, by putting a 7-wide glyph where an 8-wide one used to be — 7 is odd
and 8 is even, and a half-step scale is kinder to even widths.

### The tension, which is the actual decision

The lock's whole purpose is whole device pixels per ART pixel. On a predoubled
press that REQUIRES permitting half-integer native scales. But text lives in
native space and needs whole native scales. **The two goals are in direct conflict
on exactly the presses that predouble**, and no amount of rounding satisfies both.

Options, none free:

1. **Quantise to whole NATIVE steps whenever the frame carries rasterised text.**
   Text is always clean; art loses a rung on predoubled presses (2.5 -> 2.0), so
   the lock buys less magnification than it could.
2. **Keep half-steps, and draw text at a size the scale can carry** — e.g. render
   glyphs pre-scaled so the composite resample is 1:1 for them. Correct for both,
   and much more work; it means text no longer rides the single full-frame resize.
3. **Choose per frame**: half-steps only when nothing rasterised text into the
   composite. Fragile — the same game flips between framings turn to turn, and the
   scale would visibly jump.

Option 1 is the smallest honest fix and the one to cost first.

### Reproducing

Colour press, raster, lock ON, at a pane whose free scale lands above a half-step
rung. `/dump-windows` does NOT capture the difference — two dumps at panes
856x810 and 1040x972 came back byte-identical apart from the pane-size line, so
diagnose from rendered frames, not from the log.

## CONFIRMED on a real terminal (2026-08-22)

> "verified, once i get to the full non-0.5 multiple on the color version things
> get clear again"

That is the diagnosis above, closed. On the Macintosh COLOUR press the lock's
ladder is half-steps (`art_scale` (2,2) means one art pixel is two native pixels,
so a whole-art rung is a half-native one):

| locked native scale | art | text |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0, 2.0, 3.0 | whole device px per art px | **clean** — whole native step |
| 1.5, 2.5, 3.5 | whole device px per art px | **broken** — 7-wide glyph gets 10.5 device px |

Reproduced headlessly at two panes, which is the pair to use when fixing this:
free 1.300x locks to **1.0** and reads clean; free 1.575x locks to **1.5** and the
letterforms come apart — strokes alternate one and two device pixels, `l` and `i`
go ragged, while the compass rose and pillars in the SAME frame stay crisp. That
contrast within one image is the signature.

Three independent confirmations now agree: `graphics.rs`'s own
`the_pixel_lock_ladder_still_governs_when_the_cap_is_gone` states the half-step
ladder outright; the black-and-white press (`art_scale` (1,1), whole rungs only)
never exhibits it; and a whole multiple on the colour press restores clarity.

## What this settles about the fix

**Option 1 is the right shape** — quantise to whole NATIVE steps whenever the
frame carries rasterised text. The user's observation is direct evidence that
whole native steps work; the only cost is that a predoubled press loses a rung
(2.5 -> 2.0), so the lock buys less magnification than it could on those presses
alone. Option 2 (pre-scaling glyphs so the composite resample is 1:1 for them) is
strictly better and much larger; option 3 (choosing per frame) should be dropped —
the scale would visibly jump as a game moves between framings.

## Not a release blocker (assessment, not a decision)

It requires `v6_pixel_lock` ON, which is **off by default**, and a predoubled
press — the Macintosh colour archive today. A player who never enables the lock
never meets it, and one who does can sit on a whole rung. Worth fixing soon
because the lock is exactly the setting a player enables to make things look
BETTER, and on that press it currently trades sharp art for broken text.

## CLOSED by the user on sight, once the Macintosh face was actually being drawn (2026-08-23)

> "1012 is closed with new mac font"

**Why this is coherent rather than contradictory.** The diagnosis below predicted the opposite — that SQ-1011's crisper purpose-drawn face would make the breakdown MORE legible, not less. Two things happened between that prediction and this re-test:

1. **SQ-1011 shipped INERT twice** before it worked, so some of the original pixel-lock observation was made against `vga16` regardless of what the metrics line said.
2. **SQ-1018**: on a compilation volume the Macintosh face was never found AT ALL — `from_volume` took the first `APPL` on the platter. Any testing done on the Masterpieces CD was drawing an 8-wide `vga16` glyph CROPPED into a 7-wide cell, and then resampling that at a half-step. Two lossy steps stacked, not one.

With `FONT` 524 blitting 1:1 at the native cell, only the resample remains, and the user judges the result acceptable.

## What is NOT fixed, so nobody re-derives it

**The half-step ladder is arithmetic and is unchanged.** `art_scale` (2,2) still means a whole-art rung is a half-native one, and a 7-wide glyph at native scale 1.5 still lands on 10.5 device pixels. Nothing in SQ-1011 or SQ-1018 touched that.

So this is closed as **no longer objectionable in practice**, not as *mechanism removed*. If it is reported again — a different predoubled press, a larger pane, a face that is not 7x15 — the analysis above still stands and Option 1 (quantise to whole NATIVE steps when the frame carries rasterised text) is still the smallest honest fix, with the repro pair already measured: free 1.300 locks to 1.0 and reads clean, free 1.575 locks to 1.5.
