## The idea, and the inversion that makes it safe

The original proposal was a PRE-parser: understand a much larger vocabulary and
map it into the story's dictionary before the command reaches the game.

**Do not substitute.** A wrong rewrite does not fail loudly — it performs a
plausible WRONG action. `illuminate the room` silently becoming `burn room`
because `burn` was the nearest known verb drops a turn, possibly a life, and the
player never learns it happened. That is the same failure shape this project keeps
catching in rendering: self-consistent, confident and wrong. It also robs the
player of learning the game's own language, which is a real part of playing these.

**Be reactive instead.** Let the command through untouched. Only when the parser
REJECTS it do you reach for the larger vocabulary, and then only to OFFER:

    > illuminate the lamp
    [I don't know the word "illuminate".]
    lanthorn: this story knows  light · turn on · burn

A bad suggestion costs a keystroke rather than a turn, and the player stays in
charge of what is sent.

## Distinguish the failure kinds — we can, and they need different help

We have the dictionary AND the live object tree, so before or after a rejection we
can tell apart:

1. **the word is not in the dictionary at all** — offer near-matches
2. **the word is known but is not a verb here** — a grammar question (SQ-1040)
3. **the word is known and names an object that is not in scope** — say so plainly
   rather than offering synonyms; the player used the right word in the wrong room

Only the first is a vocabulary problem. Conflating the three is what makes
existing IF help feel stupid.

## Sourcing the larger vocabulary

`lookup` already encodes a candidate the way the game would, so matching is exact
rather than fuzzy once you have a candidate. What is needed is a synonym source.
Edit distance alone catches typos and misses `illuminate` -> `light` entirely.
WordNet is the obvious corpus and its licence is permissive; check it against the
zero-dependency rule — this lives in `app`, which may take dependencies, NOT in
zvm.

**Truncation gotcha:** v3 dictionary keys are 6 Z-characters and v4+ are 9, so
compare TRUNCATED forms. `flashlight` is stored as `flashl`, and distinct English
words genuinely collide there.

## Explicitly not

An LLM between the player and the parser. If a model belongs anywhere it is as an
optional outboard suggester on the rejection path, off by default, never in the
input path. lanthorn's whole character is that the VM crates take no dependencies
and every claim is falsifiable against real media; a model that silently
paraphrases the player is at odds with both.

Depends on SQ-1040 for anything beyond flat word matching. Related: SQ-1042.
