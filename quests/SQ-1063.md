## What the capture already settles

* **The flanks are TILED and their source is a full-height crop.** Both flank bands read `[Art, tiled]` with `source 76x1224` / `84x1224` **native px** — 1224 rows of source for a game screen that is only **390 native px tall**. So the tiler is being handed a source three times the height of the artwork and is repeating it to fill; whatever is at native y=0 — which is the **top panel** — comes round again down the column. That is exactly the reported symptom: the reversed section of the side art includes the very top, because the very top IS the top panel.
* **`into 76x1224px at (84,0) of 160x1224`** — the left flank is composed into a surface twice its own width at x-offset 84, the right at x-offset 0 of 168. That mirroring offset is worth reading closely; it is how the two flanks share one tile sheet, and a wrong offset would mirror one of them.
* **The pane is unusually tall for the frame**: 102x68 cells at 8x18 px = 816x1224 device px against a 637x390 native screen. Scale is **1.00** with `y-offset 0`, so the frame occupies the top 390 device rows and the flanks are asked to cover all 1224. The report says "in really long window", which matches — the taller the pane, the more of the column is filler.
* **`blank rows 0, longest run 0 at 0`** on both flanks, so the tiler found no blank seam to cut on and fell back to repeating the whole crop.

## Where to look

`render::v6_layout`'s flank handling and the band tiler in `render::graphics` — specifically how a flank's SOURCE crop is chosen when the destination is taller than the frame. SQ-0779 (`clear_text_columns`) and the SQ-0750 flank rules are the neighbours; `v6_side_border_tiling` is the existing suite and `extend_raster_flanks` the raster twin.

**A flank's source must be the frame's own side column and nothing above it.** The top panel is a separate strip — the dump lists it as `strip:art 61x4 at (21,1)` — and it must not be inside the flank's crop. Check whether the crop starts at native y=0 rather than at the top of the side column proper.

## FIXED — an eight-row rounded corner was turning a slab into a pillar

Reproduced on `stories/Shogun.toast` (Macintosh, **release 292 / serial 890314**) at
the reporter's **100x71**, hybrid, on the InvisiClues topic list.

### The first note's reading was wrong, and usefully so

It said the tiler was "handed a source three times the height of the artwork and is
repeating it", and pointed at the crop starting at native y=0. The crop is not the
problem — the **classification** is. Traced live, the hint frame answers:

```
flank x=[0,72) art=(0,400) kind=ZorkZeroPillars banner=58
   widths_all=(60,72)  widths_below=(60,68)
   below-panel profile: 68x2 64x2 62x4 60x334
```

Shogun's flank is a plain **60-wide slab for 334 rows**. Above it the top panel
rounds into it over **eight rows** — `68x2 64x2 62x4`. `painted_widths` took its
widest row raw, so `hi` was 68 and `lo` 60, and `recognize`'s pillar test
`lo * 10 < hi * 9` fired by **twelve pixels**. The frame was handed
`BorderArt::ZorkZeroPillars`, whose recipe found neither a shaft nor a band, fell
through to Zork Zero's own hardcoded constants (`TOP_CUT 86`, `UNIT 284`,
`flip = true`) and mirrored a 284-row unit down the column.

That is exactly the two things the reporter described — "tile/mirror past the side
art into the banner" — and exactly the failure mode SQ-0899 already named: *"he was
handed Zork Zero's masonry — the ornament tiled down the plain shaft, which is the
'banner repeating down the flank'"*.

**Why it looked like a pane-size threshold**: the extension only exists when the pane
is taller than the art, so nothing shows until it is. And the same press's GAME frame
profiles `92px x400` — no panel over those columns at all — and classified correctly
the whole time. One title, two answers, depending on which screen was up. That is why
re-checking at a different pane made it look "gone" (it was discarded on 2026-08-24
for that reason).

### The fix, in three parts

1. **`painted_widths`: a span counts only once it has held for a text row.** The same
   floor `repeats_an_ornament` already applies — *"a band thinner than a text row is
   dither, not architecture"*. Shogun's corner (2, 2 and 4 rows) stops setting `hi`,
   the slab measures `(60, 60)`, and it classifies as `ShogunSinglePiece`. Every real
   capital and ornament in the corpus is far taller — Zork Zero's capitals 82 to 292
   rows, Arthur's ornaments 34 — so nothing real is removed.
2. **`banner_height`: the top panel is not part of the flank.** Its ends sit inside
   every flank crop. It is identified by whether the paint CONTINUES INWARD past the
   flank's inner edge (`x1` for a left crop, `x0 - 1` for a right one) rather than by
   "spans the canvas", so an image cropped to the flank answers 0 and is measured as
   before. Only the SHAPE statistics move; `inset` and the `top == 0` test still read
   the art's true extent.
3. **The single-piece recipe repeats the ornament, upright.** `steady_top` cuts the
   unit where the flank's width settles — below the panel and below the corner it
   rounds through — and the mirrored second stamp is gone. That mirror is Bocfel's
   device for hiding a seam, and Shogun's ornament is DIRECTIONAL: flipped, its motifs
   hang upside down for four hundred rows. The overlap alone closes the seam.

### Verification

* `flank_shape --all` over the whole corpus — Arthur (three presses), Zork Zero (two),
  Shogun (three) — **classifies identically before and after**. This change is
  invisible to every frame that was already right.
* All 17 `render::v6_border` cases pass, including the ones that pin the shapes this
  touches (`a_slab_that_reaches_the_bottom_is_not_a_pillar`,
  `an_inset_plate_that_all_but_reaches_the_bottom_is_not_a_pillar`,
  `the_pillar_shaft_is_measured_from_the_art_not_pinned_to_one_banner_height`).
* Three new cases: `a_rounded_corner_is_not_a_capital`,
  `a_tall_capital_is_still_a_pillar` (the other side of the floor, so it cannot be
  loosened blindly), and
  `a_single_piece_extension_repeats_neither_the_panel_nor_its_corner`.
* The rendered frame changes only from device row 476 down — where the mirror seam
  was — and the flank is now upright ornament the whole way, with the panel's corner
  only at the top where it belongs.

### One caution for the next reader

`banner_height`'s first draft keyed on "the row spans the whole canvas", and that
broke three existing tests, which build a canvas **exactly the flank's width** — so
a legitimate capital read as a panel. The inward probe is not a stylistic preference;
it is what makes the test canvases and the real 640-wide screen agree.

## The cause, measured

`stories/Shogun.toast` (Macintosh, **release 292 / serial 890314**), hybrid, on the
InvisiClues topic list at the reporter's **100x71**. Traced live:

```
flank x=[0,72) art=(0,400) kind=ZorkZeroPillars
   widths_all=(60,72)  widths_below=(60,68)
   below-panel profile: 68x2 64x2 62x4 60x334
```

Shogun's flank is a **plain 60-wide lattice of question marks, 334 rows**. Above it
the frame's top panel rounds into the column over **eight rows** — `68x2 64x2 62x4`.
`painted_widths` takes the widest row raw, so `hi = 68` against `lo = 60`, and
`recognize`'s pillar test `lo * 10 < hi * 9` fires **by twelve pixels**. The flank is
classified `ZorkZeroPillars`.

`zork_zero()` then finds no shaft (`pillar_shaft` wants a base under the band and
this one runs to the bottom) and no banded shaft, and falls through to **Zork Zero's
own hardcoded constants** — `TOP_CUT 86`, `FOOT 26`, `UNIT 284`, `flip = true`. Those
are measurements of a different drawing. Applied here they mirror four hundred rows
of ornament and reprint the frame's panel down the column: precisely
"tile/mirror past the side art into the banner".

**Why it looked like a pane-size threshold**: the extension only exists when the pane
is taller than the art. And the same press's GAME frame profiles `92px x400` — no
panel over those columns — and has always classified correctly. One title, two
answers, depending on which screen is up; that is why re-checking at another pane
made it look "gone" and got it discarded on 2026-08-24.

The first note's reading ("the crop starts at native y=0") was wrong. The crop is
fine; the CLASSIFICATION is wrong, and the recipe then borrows another game's
constants.

## Six attempts, and what killed each

Every one produced the CORRECT render on the reported frame. Each was reverted
because an existing test caught a regression the render could not show.

| # | attempt | killed by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `painted_widths`: a span counts only after holding a text row | `v6_archive_border_sweep` property 6 — `zork0.mg1` plate 6's two crops classified differently (pillars vs slab), and the tally moved 12 pillars → 8 pillars + 4 slabs |
| 2 | `banner_height`: skip a leading full-canvas-width band | three existing `v6_border` unit tests build a canvas **exactly the flank's width**, so a real capital read as a panel |
| 3 | `banner_height` by probing INWARD (paint continues past the flank's inner edge) | Zork Zero's banner and its capitals are CONTIGUOUS — the probe walks straight through the capital |
| 4 | measure stats from `steady_top` (where the width settles) | walks past a real capital; four `zork0.mg1` plates became slabs |
| 5 | bound that skip at one text row | same four plates; their capitals settle within a text row |
| 6 | replace `zork_zero`'s constants fallback with "repeat this flank's own band" | the constants are LOAD-BEARING — `zork0.mg1` plate 6/8 and the `.adf` reach that arm and need the **FOOT** it restores (SQ-0841). Continuing the band leaves bare shaft below the foot |

Attempt 6 was then narrowed on the reporter's own suggestion — *"detect that the
middle 18 rows of pixels are replicated, so we just continue that replication"* — as
an `ornament_period` arm tried BEFORE the constants: find the smallest `p` for which
every row equals the row `p` above it, holding for at least three periods and ending
at the art's bottom, then tile the last whole period. **That renders correctly and in
phase** — the lattice continues cleanly the whole way down. It still failed the sweep:
Zork Zero's **plate 8** has a masonry shaft regular enough to satisfy it, and
continuing it drops that plate's foot. Two further tightenings did not separate them:

* requiring one constant painted width across the whole repeating stretch (a
  lattice has one width; a pillar's foot flares) — plate 8 still passes;
* additionally requiring the repeat to cover 60% of the column — plate 8 still passes.

## Where that leaves it

The periodic arm is the right idea and needs a discriminator that plate 8 fails.
Get the numbers before writing another one: **print plate 8's row-period, the extent
over which it holds, and its painted-span profile, beside Shogun's**, and find what
actually differs. Everything above was written without that comparison, which is why
six attempts each died to a different plate.

Two other routes not yet tried:

1. Fix the CLASSIFICATION with the panel measured from the frame rather than from the
   flank — `recognize` sees only `[x0, x1)`, but the caller has the whole canvas and
   knows where the top strip is (`chrome ring strips` already reports it). Every
   attempt above tried to infer the panel from inside the crop, which is what kept
   colliding with Zork Zero's contiguous capital. The strip is known outright at the
   call site.
2. Give the periodic arm to `ShogunSinglePiece` instead, and let it apply only when
   the flank was NOT classified as pillars — no help on its own, since the misclass
   is the defect, but it composes with (1).

## The three-section model — the reporter's framing, and it is the right one

> "there are three separate artwork frame sections: the banner, middle section
> (repeats/tiles), and footer. Combinations: 0. no artwork (some Arthur Fx screens);
> 1. banner only; 2. middle only (Shogun); 3. banner+middle (Arthur, hint screens);
> 4. banner+middle+footer (Zork Zero)."

This dissolves the question every earlier attempt was stuck on. Those attempts all
asked *"which of three games drew this flank"*, which is why each discriminator
collided with a different plate. The section model never asks: extending a flank is
one operation — **keep the banner, re-anchor the FOOTER to the new bottom, tile the
middle between them** — and the title never enters into it.

It also explains the last failure outright. The reporter's own earlier suggestion
("detect that the middle rows are replicated and continue that replication") rendered
correctly and failed the sweep for exactly one reason: it continued the middle past
the bottom and dropped Zork Zero's foot. Under this model the footer is a section in
its own right that is always re-anchored, so that cannot happen.

## Implemented (UNCOMMITTED, working tree)

`v6_border::FlankSections` + `flank_sections()` + `extend_by_sections()`, with
`flank_source` routed through it. The three per-title recipes (`arthur`, `zork_zero`,
`shogun`) are now dead code, retained only until this lands.

**A middle is found two ways, because there are two kinds of middle** — this is the
part that took the longest to see:

* a **LATTICE** repeats in the PIXELS (Shogun's question marks, Zork Zero's plate 8).
  Only periodicity finds it; silhouette statistics cannot, which is what read
  Shogun's eight-row rounded corner as a capital in the first place.
* a **SHAFT** is a plain span of one width between a capital and a base. It repeats
  trivially, so periodicity returns a meaningless 4-row unit — its EXTENT is the
  information, and `pillar_shaft`/`banded_shaft` have measured exactly that over
  sixty-eight flanks since SQ-0792/SQ-0841.

## What the corpus says — the model derives the hardcoded constants

Measured over all 68 flanks (`SEC=1 cargo nextest run -p app
the_corpus_states_sixty_eight_flanks --no-capture`, with a temporary print):

| flank | sections |
|---|---|
| Arthur (3 presses) | banner ~174 · middle to the bottom · **no footer** — case 3 |
| Zork Zero **plate 5** (castle) | banner 0..**82** · middle 82..374 · footer 374..400 (**26**) — case 4 |
| Zork Zero **plate 8** (InvisiClues) | banner 0..58/66 · middle p=**40** · no footer — case 3 |
| Shogun game frames (mg1/eg1/cg1/adf) | **middle only**, no banner, no footer — case 2 |
| Shogun **hint** frame (`Shogun.toast`) | banner 0..**66** · middle p=**40** · no footer |

**82 and 26 are `TOP_CUT` (less its 4-row `INSET`) and `FOOT`** — the two Bocfel
constants `zork_zero()` hardcodes. The model measures them out of the art. That is
the strongest evidence the abstraction is the right one, and it is symmetric across
left and right crops on every rendition of plate 5 (`zork0.mg1`, `.eg1`, `.cg1`,
`Pic.data`, `.adf`).

Shogun's hint banner comes out **0..66** — the 58-row panel plus the eight rows of
corner that the whole defect turned on — found automatically rather than tuned.

## What is NOT done

`v6_archive_border_sweep` still fails two cases, both on Zork Zero's **scene plates 6
and 7** — the underground and jungle borders, which the sweep's own note says no
affordable play session reaches. They are organic drawings with **neither a period
nor a shaft**, so they land in the `(None, None)` arm, and the footer probe there is
unstable: adjacent columns of the same plate report footers of 30 / 0 / 32 / 0, and
plate 6's right crop starts at art row 2 where its left starts at 0, so the two sides
disagree (SQ-0845's property 6).

The remaining work is a footer detector for art the model cannot otherwise section.
The modal-painted-width test currently used is too fragile; note that plate 6's foot
does **not** flare — it is the same width as the shaft and differs only in content —
so width alone will not find it. Consider the last significant span change within a
bounded distance of the bottom, and check it on BOTH crops of plates 6 and 7 across
all five renditions before believing it.

Do not resolve this by falling back to `zork_zero()`'s constants for unsectionable
art: Shogun's GAME border is also unsectionable (no period, no shaft — it is one
drawing), and handing it Zork Zero's masonry is the original defect wearing a new
hat.
