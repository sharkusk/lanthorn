## Why it blends today

`render::tooltip::draw_tip_on` defaults to `BorderStyle::None` and fills with
`tooltip.background` and nothing else. A frame is already possible — a themed
`tooltip.border` selector turns it on — but off by default, so the tip is a
rectangle of slightly-different background with no edge and no relationship to
the thing it explains.

**One implementation already serves everything** (lifted out of `debug_panel`
when the border controls needed the same box, SQ-1123): the debugger's value tip
and every border-control hint clamp, frame and paint through it, reading the same
`tooltip.background` / `tooltip.border` selectors. So this lands everywhere at
once, the coming help overlay included.

## The technique the user asked for, and why it works

> "Is there a way to do a solid triangle that blends off the top/bottom of a solid
> box as the pointer?"

Yes, and the mechanism is that the pointer is **not a character drawn on the
box** — it is a glyph whose FOREGROUND is the box's background colour:

```
cell at the anchor column, in the row between icon and box:
    ch = '▲'
    fg = the colour tooltip.background resolves to    ← the triangle IS the box
    bg = whatever is behind it                        ← page shows through around it
```

The row directly below is the box's solid top row in that same colour, so the
triangle's base meets it with no seam. It stops reading as a glyph and starts
reading as the box tapering to a point.

## Two forms to render and choose between BY EYE

* **One cell, `▲`/`▼`.** Safest — universal font coverage. Flaw: most fonts do not
  draw the triangle edge-to-edge, so at larger sizes a hairline of page colour can
  show between its base and the box.
* **Two cells, `◢◣` (and `◥◤` inverted).** Quadrant triangles fill exactly half a
  cell and are designed to tile, so `◢` rising right followed by `◣` falling right
  gives one triangle two cells wide, apex at the join, unbroken base. Wider and
  more emphatic, which suits a pointer aimed at a single icon.

**The hairline question cannot be settled from a mockup** — it depends on the
user's font at their size. Render both against their real theme on a real border
control and let them pick.

## This forces the SOLID box, not the framed one

A framed tooltip puts a line border between the pointer and the fill, so the
result is two adjacent shapes — exactly what this is trying to get away from. Of
the four variants sketched, this is the solid-fill one (no line border, contrast
carried by colour), not the notch-in-a-frame one.

A drop shadow — cells one column right and one row below painted with a darkened
background — composes with it and is what actually guarantees separation from
whatever is underneath, independent of how close the two backgrounds happen to be.
Truecolor on the user's terminals (Ghostty, Windows Terminal 1.22+); degrades to a
dim block at 16 colours.

## Three things that decide the implementation

1. **It costs one row.** The tip starts at `row + 1` today; a protruding pointer
   takes that row and the box starts at `row + 2`. On a cramped pane the graceful
   degradation is to drop the POINTER and keep the tip, never to lose the tip.
2. **The pointer flips with the box.** `draw_tip_on` already flips `TipSide` when
   the preferred side would overrun; the pointer must flip with it — `▲`/`◢◣` on
   top when below, `▼`/`◥◤` on the bottom when above.
3. **The pointer must track the anchor after clamping, and stay honest.** The box
   slides left at the right edge, so the pointer's column is `anchor − box.x`.
   When the anchor falls outside the box's span, draw **no pointer** rather than
   one aimed at nothing — a tooltip pointing somewhere misleading is worse than
   one that does not point at all.

## Styling

Every new element needs a `style.toml` selector; that is the standing rule. A
`tooltip.pointer` glyph (inheriting the fill colour) and a shadow selector give a
free answer for terminals with poor geometric coverage: theme the pointer down to
`▲` → `^` → nothing without touching code.

## Sequencing

Deliberately BEFORE the help hotkey, at the user's direction: an overlay showing
many hints at once magnifies whatever the single-hint style gets wrong.
