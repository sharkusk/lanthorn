## CORRECTION: this is a RENDER defect, not the window geometry

An earlier note on this quest reasoned that the full-width band and SQ-1029's
truncated side art shared one cause — `@split_window` resetting windows 0 and 1
to the full screen width — and that fixing the split would fix both. **Half of
that was wrong, and the measurements say so plainly.**

The split fix landed (windows now tile VERTICALLY, keeping the horizontal box the
game set). With it in place:

* **Shogun, gameplay.** The model is right: `win1 = (47, 1, 548, 32)`, inset
  between the ornaments, exactly the box the game asks for and the machine draws.
  A pty capture at 132x60 still paints the band **edge to edge**, above the
  flanks, where `machine-screenshots/amiga-shogun-game.png` has it 540 px wide
  from native x 47 with the ornaments running up beside it.
* **Arthur, the F2 map screen.** The model is right there too, and it was never
  touched by the split: `win1 = (29, 201, 584, 20)`, with the side poles as
  `win5 = (1, 1, 28, 400)` and `win6 = (613, 1, 28, 400)`. No `split_window` is
  issued on that turn at all. `machine-screenshots/amiga-arthur-map.png` measures
  its white bar at image x 71..652 — **582 px** — which against a 28-px pole is
  `640 - 2*29`, i.e. between the poles. Ours spans the full width.

Two different games, two different routes, one of them with no `split_window`
involved, both with a correctly-inset window 1 in the model and a full-width band
on screen. So the defect is **below the model, in the render**, and the user's
own read — \"these could be unrelated\" — is the right one.

## Where to look

The band reaching the pane edges while its window does not is either the chrome
strip the hybrid ring cuts for the status row, or a background flood painted per
ROW rather than per window box (`fill_explicit_bg_rows` and the erase-fill ground
are the candidates named in `render::v6_layout`). Establish which before
changing anything: a strip drawn too wide and a ground flooded too wide look
identical on screen and are different fixes — the same distinction SQ-0762's pty
harness exists to make.

The user notes it on the **F2–F6 screens** in Arthur specifically, so sweep those
rather than only the gameplay frame.

## Diagnosed: the flank claims columns its ART does not occupy

Traced through `render::screen`'s ring carver on the reported frame.

`ChromeRowOracle::blocked` decides whether a flank may own a row. Its own comment
names this press:

> A BAR reaches as far as its own WINDOW and no further (SQ-0949) — and only a bar
> takes this branch. […] **Shogun's own status band is 548 of 640 native columns,
> under the bar threshold**, with its first glyph straddling the flank's last cell
> — measured on `James Clavell's Shogun.adf` (release 295) at a 70-column pane,
> judging that row by its window instead lost the `E` of `Erasmus:` to the flank.

So the band is judged by where its GLYPHS stand, its first glyph lands inside the
flank's columns, the flank is vetoed off those rows, and the top strip is cut
across the whole pane — which is the full-width band on screen.

**The glyph is only inside the flank because the flank is too wide.**
`content_ring_bands` takes `left_cols = (pane.x, viewport.x)` — everything left of
the story viewport — and at a 132x60 pane that is about 15 cells. Shogun's
ornament is **46 native px**, about 9 cells at that letterbox scale. The six cells
between are bare ground the flank claims but does not paint, and `Erasmus:` begins
at native 47, inside them.

`flank_art_columns` already measures the art's own width, exactly for this class of
problem ("So ask the art", SQ-0894) — but it is only used to SHRINK THE VIEWPORT
(`viewport.x.max(art_left)`), never to bound the flank itself. The flank is still
"whatever the story box leaves", which is the very thing that quest set out to fix.

## Two candidate fixes, and why the first is preferred

1. **Bound the flank by its art.** Give `content_ring_bands` the `(art_left,
   art_right)` pair it already has to hand and use those for `left_cols` /
   `right_cols` instead of the viewport's complement. Then the band's first glyph
   at native 47 is outside the flank, nothing is vetoed, and the ornament keeps
   the rows beside the band — which is what
   `machine-screenshots/amiga-shogun-game.png` shows. It also directly finishes
   SQ-0894's stated intent.
2. **Judge the row by its window span.** Already rejected once and the rejection
   is recorded above: it cost the `E` of `Erasmus:` at a 70-column pane. Do not
   re-litigate without re-measuring that pane.

## Arthur agrees, and gives the width to aim at

`machine-screenshots/amiga-arthur-map.png`: the score bar measures image x 71..652
— **582 px** — against 28-px poles, i.e. `640 - 2*29`, sitting between them.
Arthur's model is already right (`win1 = (29, 201, 584, 20)`), so his full-width
band has the same cause: a flank claiming columns out to the story box rather than
out to its poles.

## Watch for

`journey_flank_border_is_drawn_at_the_letterbox_scale` (the doubled-rule case a
too-WIDE flank was introduced to avoid), the 70-column Shogun pane named above,
and `a_flanks_columns_come_from_the_narrowest_row_of_its_art`. Sweep panes: the
symptom is pane-dependent because the flank's width is.

| press | window 1's box | the art beside it |
|---|---|---|
| Shogun, gameplay | `(47, 1, 548, 32)` | ornament 46 native px wide |

Both are the game's own, stated explicitly (`move_window(win=1, y=1, x=47)` /
`window_size(win=1, y=32, x=548)`), and both already agree with the machine —
Shogun's band measures 540 px from native 47 on `amiga-shogun-game.png`, Arthur's
582 px from native 29 on `amiga-arthur-map.png`.

**They ABUT the art exactly.** Shogun's ornament occupies native 0..46 and
`Erasmus` starts at native **47**; Arthur's poles end at 28 and his band starts at
29. There is no overlap between the band's window and the side art on either
press.

That reframes the rejected alternative recorded above. The `E` of `Erasmus:` was
not lost because window 1 overlaps the ornament — it does not — but because the

narrow the flank to its art and the question does not arise.

* the band's WINDOW says the flank may have everything left of native 47;
* the band's ART says the flank is 46 wide.

Only the viewport-derived flank disagrees with both. Bounding `content_ring_bands`'
`left_cols` / `right_cols` by `flank_art_columns` satisfies the window box and the
art at once, and needs no change to `blocked`'s glyph rule — which keeps the
70-column Shogun pane that rule was written for.

## The v1-5 assumption, found — the bar flood ignores the window's box

The user's question: *\"Are we falling into the logic for v3-v5 games where we may
be extending the score bar reverse to cover the entire width?\"* Yes, on one press.

`render::screen::draw_painted_screen`:

```rust
let flood = full_width_flood_rows(chrome, native_w, base, ink, cell);
for (&row, &style) in &flood {
    …
    for x in area.x..area.right() {      // <- the WHOLE terminal row
```

A row classified as a bar is flooded **edge to edge across the pane**, with no
reference to the box of the window that owns it. In Versions 1-5 that is right —
the status line IS the screen's width, so a terminal row is exactly its extent. In
Version 6 a window has a box, and the two part company.

Which rows qualify, from `full_width_flood_rows`:

```rust
let threshold = native_w as u32 * 9 / 10;      // >= 90% of the native width
```

| press | status window | of 640 | bar? | machine's band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arthur (Amiga r54), F2 map | `(29, 201, 584, 20)` | **91.25%** | **YES** | 582 px, between 28-px poles |
| Shogun (Amiga r295), gameplay | `(47, 1, 548, 32)` | 85.6% | no | 540 px, between 46-px ornaments |

So **Arthur's band is flooded across the whole pane** when its own window stops 28
columns short of each edge — which is exactly where his poles stand, and exactly
what `machine-screenshots/amiga-arthur-map.png` shows. That is this quest's
symptom on that press, and it is a different mechanism from the flank-columns one
diagnosed above for Shogun (which is under the threshold and never reaches this
code at all).

**Two presses, two mechanisms, one symptom.** Both must be fixed for the band to
sit between the artwork on both games:

1. **Arthur** — bound the flood to the owning window's span instead of the pane.
   `row_spans` (SQ-0949) already computes native row → the union of its owning
   windows' spans, and today it is fed ONLY to the flank veto
   (`ChromeRowOracle::blocked`). The same fact bounds the flood.
2. **Shogun** — bound the flank to its art (`flank_art_columns`) instead of to the
   story viewport's complement, per the note above.

Both are the same shape of error: a fact that exists and is measured, consulted in
one place and ignored in another.

## Watch for

`advent.z6` is the reason the flood's second arm exists — it paints
\"At End Of Road … Score: 36 … Moves: 1\" into a full-width, one-row window with no
reverse bit, and the theme's bar background reached only the cells under the

case is unaffected — but it is the case to re-measure first.

Zork Zero's `\" InvisiClues (tm)\"` header is likewise a 640/640 window and stays
full width.

## Attempted and REVERTED — three sites found, no rule that fits all of them

The v1-5 assumption the user asked about is real and it is in **three** places, each
independently flooding a bar wider than its window:

| site | bound | layer |
|---|---|---|
| `screen::draw_painted_screen` | `area.x..area.right()` | cell path |
| `v6_layout::fill_reverse_row_gaps` | `canvas.width()` | native chrome canvas |
| `screen::draw_chrome_text_strip` | the strip's rect (clamped to the game screen, SQ-0946) | hybrid ring |

Bounding the second by the owning window WORKS at the canvas level and matches the
machine: Arthur's band row went `opaque 0..640` to `opaque 28..612` — **584 px
against a 584-px window**, where three captures of that machine
(`amiga-arthur-map.png`, `amiga-arthur-inventory.png`, `amiga-arthur-church.png`)
all measure the band at **582 px, image x 71..652**. Worth noting that
`fill_reverse_row_gaps`' own comment cites the inventory capture as showing the
band \"filled edge to edge\"; it does not, and the other two agree with it.

**But it does not survive the corpus.** `v6_arthur_hint_page::the_hint_screen_is_one_page_and_not_a_row_of_islands`
fails: *\"row 0 shows the ink on 80 of 100 cells — a reversed band is the whole row
or none of it\"*. That case is SQ-1004's, and its rule is a PROXY for the islands
defect (a row becoming as many blocks as it had words) rather than a width the
machine pins. Measured on `amiga-arthur-hint.png`, the machine's top rows carry
content only at image 264..419 — a small centred title box, neither the full row
nor 80% of it — so the capture does not settle which of the two lanthorn should
draw, and the proxy cannot tell a contiguous 80% band from islands.

So: the window bound makes the STATUS band right and turns a pinned invariant on
the HINT page into something that rule cannot judge. Reverted rather than shipped.
The tree is back at HEAD.

## And the third site cannot use a window bound at all

`draw_chrome_text_strip` renders the band that is actually on screen — measured,
`strip:text(75 runs) 85x1@(0,12)`, the full pane width, and reading the row back
cell by cell gives one reversed style across all 85 columns.

Neither available fact bounds it. The strip's `runs` are gathered **by row across
every chrome window**, and Arthur's map screen carries a full-screen grid
(`native (0, 0, 640, 400)`) alongside its 584-px status window, so BOTH the runs'
own hull AND `row_spans` (SQ-0949) come back 0..640. Deciding which window's runs
constitute the bar is a design question, not a bound.

## What the next attempt needs

1. **A machine capture that settles Arthur's HINT page top row** — is it a
   full-width band, a 584-px band, or the small centred box the existing capture
   shows? Until that is known, no rule can be checked against both pages.
2. **A way to name the bar's window from inside a strip.** The strip would have to
   carry its runs' provenance rather than just the runs; that is the change, and it
   should be judged on its own before any flood is re-bounded.
3. Re-read SQ-1004's proxy afterwards: \"whole row or none\" may itself need to
   become \"one contiguous band\", which would admit a correctly-bounded bar.

## ROOT INSIGHT: the window box is a STALE bound, and that is why entries differ

The reporter noticed the reverse spans the whole width on the SECOND entry to the
hint menu but not the first, and guessed the code path differed. It does not. The
window box does.

Their `/dump-windows` on the second entry (Macintosh `Shogun.toast`, raster):

```
win0  500x330 at (71,71)      <- still inset
win1  640x60  at (1,1)        <- FULL WIDTH
```

against the first entry's `win1 500x70 at (71,1)`. Reproduced headlessly with
`hint_probe --route 'T:3;L:hint;C:y;C:q;L:hint' --windows --trace`, which gives the
same `w1 box=640x60@(1,1)` and the reason:

```
@window_size(win=1, y=70, x=500)    <- the header window the menu is PAINTED into
@window_size(win=0, y=330, x=500)
@window_size(win=1, y=60, x=640)    <- and then the game WIDENS it, immediately
@window_size(win=7, y=400, x=640)
```

The game paints its InvisiClues header into a **500-wide** window and resizes that
window to **640** on the very next instruction. Both entries run the identical
sequence; the difference is only WHERE in it the frame is sampled. Render before
the widen and window 1 is 500; render after and it is 640 — with the same runs, at
the same painted positions, on screen either way.

## What that settles

**A window's current box cannot bound a bar**, because v6 text is PAINT: ZMSD
§8.8.4 — a run "does not change the current display" when its window later moves or
resizes. `cpu::exec`'s own paint path says the same in its comment, that "bounding
every run by its window's current box would erase text the game means to be on
screen", and `fill_explicit_bg_rows` already unions the window with the runs' span
for exactly this reason.

So the window-bound attempt recorded above was not merely refuted by Arthur's hint
page — it was unsound in principle, and this frame is the proof. It would produce
582 px or 640 px for the same screen depending on which instruction the renderer
happened to catch.

That leaves the runs' own hull as the only stable bound. Which Journey's header
refutes (its two labels want the whole row filled between and around them), so the
tension is now sharp and correctly located:

| frame | runs' hull | wants |
|---|---|---|
| Arthur status band | ~582 px (padded with reversed spaces) | 582 |
| Arthur InvisiClues title | ~156 px | ~156 |
| Shogun header, either entry | the header text | its own extent |
| **Journey menu header** | two labels, gap between | **640 — beyond both** |

## Where to look next

Journey is the only frame that wants more than its runs, and `dos-journey-2.png`
measures it at a genuine full-width bar (rows 304..319, 596–638 light px of 640).
So the question is no longer \"how wide is a bar\" but \"what makes Journey's row
different\", and the honest candidates are its RUN COUNT (two labels with a wide
gap, against one isolated block) or the fact that its window has not been resized
out from under it.

The second is newly testable: if Journey's menu window is still the box its runs
were painted into, while Shogun's has been widened past them, then \"the window
bounds the bar ONLY while it still contains its own runs\" fits every row in the
table. That is one measurement away.

## MEASUREMENT: `spans_window` answers correctly, and is STABLE across entries

Took the reach-both-edges test `fill_explicit_bg_rows` already uses —

```rust
let spans_window = lo <= ox + font_w && hi + font_w >= ox + win_w;
// "A window is the bar only when its runs REACH BOTH OF ITS EDGES"
```

— and measured it on every PURE-REVERSE row of the reported frames.

**Macintosh `Shogun.toast`, first entry (`T:3;L:hint;C:y`) and second entry
(`T:3;L:hint;C:y;C:q;L:hint`) — byte-identical results:**

```
win box  70..570   row y=71  runs  70..129   reaches-both-edges=false   "Erasmus| "
win box   0..640   row y=1   runs 266..374   reaches-both-edges=false   "InvisiClues (tm)| "
```

Both answer **false**, so neither row would be flooded — the topic highlight stays
around its word and the title stays a centred block, which is what the machine
shows and what the reporter says the FIRST entry looks like.

**And the two entries measure the same.** That is the important half: the defect
reported is that the second entry differs from the first, and this test does not
differ. It is immune to the stale-window-box problem recorded above, because a
window widened out from under its runs stops reaching them and so stops being a
bar — which is exactly the right answer rather than a lucky one.

Note the title row sits in a **full-width window** (0..640) with a centred run in
both entries. So "the window is full width" is NOT sufficient to make a bar; the
runs have to fill it. That is precisely the distinction the earlier window-bound
attempt lacked, and why it produced 640 where the machine shows ~156.
