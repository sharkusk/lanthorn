The controls themselves are accepted — glyphs, state, tooltips, click dispatch,
v6 gating all stand. **Only the placement changes**, and it splits the cluster:

| control | from | to |
|---|---|---|
| command band | top border, right | **bottom border, centred** |
| guidance | top border, right | **bottom border, centred** |
| v6 render mode | top border, right | **bottom border, centred** |
| v6 pixel lock | top border, right | **bottom border, centred** |
| map | top border, right | **right border, vertically centred** |

## The principle behind it, worth stating so it survives

**A control sits where the thing it governs is, or where it would appear.** The
command band opens below the story pane, so its toggle belongs on the bottom
border. The map lives to the right, so its toggle belongs on the right edge. That
is the same reasoning the arrow glyphs already encode — `▲`/`▼` for a panel below,
`◀`/`▶` for one to the side — and the original single-cluster placement fought it.

Guidance and the two v6 controls are not panels and have no direction of their
own, so they take the bottom row beside the band toggle as a natural home for
"things you switch".

## The hazard the first pass already had to dodge

SQ-1123 kept the cluster clear of the splitter's drag zone (`story.right()-1`, 2
columns wide). **The map toggle is now being deliberately placed on that very
edge**, so the collision has to be solved rather than avoided: a vertically
centred control on the right border sits exactly where a vertical splitter is
dragged. Decide how a click is disambiguated — the control taking priority over
the drag start within its one cell is the obvious answer, but it must be
deliberate and tested, and a drag begun *on* the control must not be swallowed.

The bottom border may have its own occupants; check before reserving columns, and
keep the centred group honest when the pane is too narrow to hold it (SQ-1123
drops the cluster below ~15 columns rather than showing half of one — same rule
applies per group now that there are two).

## What must not regress

The suite from the first pass (`crates/app/tests/suites/border_controls.rs`, 12
cases) pins the state glyphs, the v6 gating, the tooltip placement rules and that
every control resolves to a real `slash::COMMANDS` entry. Those assertions stay
true; only the ones about *position* should change.

## Correction: the v6 pair stays put (user, 2026-08-28)

> actually, slight correction. leave the render/pixel lock where it is located today.

Supersedes the table in the note above:

| control | to |
|---|---|
| command band | **bottom border, centred** |
| guidance | **bottom border, centred** |
| v6 render mode | **unchanged — top border, right** |
| v6 pixel lock | **unchanged — top border, right** |
| map | **right border, vertically centred** |

So the top-right cluster is not emptied but **reduced to the two v6 controls** —
and on a non-v6 story it vanishes entirely, since those are the only two left
there.

**This reads better against the principle, not worse.** A control sits where the
thing it governs is: the band opens below, so its toggle is on the bottom border;
the map lives right, so its toggle is on the right edge; guidance has no
direction of its own and joins the band. And the two v6 controls govern **how the
story pane itself is drawn** — they act on that pane rather than on anything
appearing elsewhere — so its own top border is exactly right for them.

**One consequence to state rather than discover:** a shorter (or absent) top
cluster reserves fewer columns in the title strip, which changes how much room a
long title has. That interacts with [[SQ-1127]] — `render_overflow` clips a long
title to the buffer rather than its rect, and SQ-1123's first pass mitigated it
by painting the cluster after the strip. Fewer reserved columns changes the
worst case in both directions; say what it now is.
