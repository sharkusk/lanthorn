## What is missing

`app::style::write_font_check_answer(path, true)` — what a `yes` to `/run-font-check`
writes — sets `control_icons`, `arrow_set`, `portal_icons` and the rest from the
answer. It does NOT set the six `badge_*` keys (`badge_zcode`, `badge_glulx`, …).
They are `[elements]` keys with a plain-letter default (`Z`, `G`, `S`, …) and there
is no `nerdfont` preset behind them, so a player who answers yes to the font check
gets patched glyphs everywhere EXCEPT the story-type badges in the picker.

Found by the SQ-1152 gallery lane, which was asked to render every shot with Nerd
Font icons and did — the map's arrows, the portal stairs and the pane-border
controls all became patched glyphs, and the badges stayed `Z`/`B`/`S`. The lane
correctly DECLINED to invent six codepoints in the capture harness and raised it
instead.

## The rule this has to obey

**Every Nerd Font codepoint is read from the font's own `post`/`cmap` table, never
from a cheat sheet** (SQ-1045). That is the whole of the difficulty here and the
reason this is a quest rather than six lines: picking six plausible glyph names out
of a Nerd Font reference is exactly the failure that rule exists to prevent, and a
badge that renders as tofu in the picker is worse than a letter that renders.

So: choose candidates from the patched font's own tables, verify each resolves,
and prefer a glyph whose SEMANTICS survive being three pixels tall in a list row.
A story-type badge is read at a glance beside a filename; a detailed icon and a
blank square look the same at that size.

## Scope

The preset belongs in the APP — a `nerdfont` arm for the badge elements, written by
`write_font_check_answer` alongside the others, so a player's yes covers them and
the gallery inherits it for free. Not a gallery change: the harness already calls
the app's own writer precisely so the two cannot drift.

Worth deciding while there: whether `badge_*` should be one preset key like
`control_icons` (plain | nerdfont) rather than six free-text element keys, which is
what makes them able to fall out of step in the first place.

## Not urgent

Letters are legible and correct; nothing is broken. This is consistency — every
other icon in the app follows the font check and these do not.

Related: [[SQ-1152]] is where it surfaced; SQ-1123 added the pane-border control
icons that DO have the preset; SQ-1104 is the font check itself.

## Done on `worktree-agent-a997d97fb077af15f` (e7e6d257) — awaiting the user's eye

**The shape chosen: ONE preset key, not six.** `[elements] badge_icons` (plain |
nerdfont) names a `symbols::StoryBadges` preset; `write_font_check_answer` writes
that single key alongside `[map]`'s three; the six `badge_*` keys survive as
per-slot overrides that outrank the preset. Six independent keys is what let them
fall out of step with the font check, and a NAME (rather than six expanded glyphs)
is what stops today's codepoints being frozen into the user's file — the same
reason the other three keys are names.

`StoryBadges::PLAIN` is now the single source of the `config::default_badge_*`
values, so a default cannot drift from the preset meant to hold it.

## The codepoints, and how each was verified

Read from the patched font's own `cmap`, under the font's own glyph name, across
the **nine** patched families installed on this machine (0xProto, FiraCode ×3,
IosevkaTerm, JetBrainsMono, ProggyClean, SauceCodePro, Symbols Nerd Font Mono) —
never from a cheat sheet (SQ-1045's rule, SQ-1141's method). The reader was
validated first by reproducing every previously-verified codepoint in the repo
(`fa-map_location` U+0EE68, `cod-layout_panel` U+0EBF2, `md-post_lamp` U+F1A60,
`ple-lower_right_triangle` U+0E0BA, …) before any new name was trusted.

| key | font's own name | codepoint | 9/9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `badge_zcode` | `md-alpha_z_box` | U+F0B21 | yes |
| `badge_glulx` | `md-alpha_g_box` | U+F0B0E | yes |
| `badge_blorb` | `md-package_variant_closed` | U+F03D7 | yes |
| `badge_save` | `md-content_save` | U+F0193 | yes |
| `badge_hint` | `md-lightbulb` | U+F0335 | yes |
| `badge_hint_available` | `md-lightbulb_outline` | U+F0336 | yes |

Three checks, not one: (1) the NAME resolves in all nine families at the SAME
codepoint; (2) the reverse lookup finds exactly ONE name per codepoint in every
family, so no aliasing; (3) each glyph was RASTERISED (FreeType, 12/14/16px) and
has real ink in all nine — a blank or `.notdef` outline could not pass.

## Why these glyphs and not prettier ones

- The story TYPE stays a **letter** in the patched set. Nothing in any icon font
  depicts a Z-machine or a Glulx VM, and an invented picture says LESS than the
  letter it replaced; the boxed alphabet glyphs are the letter drawn as a chip.
  `md-alpha_z_box_outline` (U+F0C36) was rejected on the rasteriser: at 12px its
  stroke is one pixel and the letter inside it goes.
- `md-archive` (U+F003C) draws a heavier silhouette than the package but is a
  lidded box, near enough to the floppy at this size to be worth avoiding.
- The hint slot's two states are ONE shape differing in FILL, because that is the
  distinction being drawn — a hint you have against one you could fetch. Same
  reading `H`/`h` carried, same filled-is-settled grammar as the portal icons'
  ◉/◎.
- All six are Material Design, so the font check's sample row needs no new slot:
  the row already samples MDI, and a face that draws that draws these. That is
  the argument `NERD_CONTROLS` already makes.

## Guards

`symbols::tests::nerdfont_badge_glyphs_are_the_names_that_were_read_from_the_font`
pins all six numbers with their names, that the set is one family, that none
estimates double-width and that the six are distinct;
`plain_badges_are_the_letters_and_the_config_defaults_come_from_them` ties the
`config::default_badge_*` values to `PLAIN`; four `style.rs` cases cover preset →
six glyphs, a hand-set key beating the preset, an unknown name falling back to
the letters, and per-game layering; `font_check::a_yes_reaches_the_picker_badges_too`
and `a_badge_the_user_chose_survives_both_answers` cover the round trip through
the real seeded file. Falsified: reverting the writer's `[elements]` block fails
the first with the reported symptom (the Z-code badge still reads `Z` after a yes).

## Finding, out of scope, NOT fixed

`badge_zcode`, `badge_glulx` and `badge_blorb` are **currently drawn nowhere**.
SQ-0369 moved the story type and the Blorb wrapper into the TYPE column as a text
label (`Z5 (blorb)`), and `picker_ui.rs` draws only `save` and `hint`/
`hint_available` in the badge cluster — `picker_ui.rs:3751` pins that on purpose.
`picker::BadgeGlyphs` still exposes all six and `style.toml` still documents all
six, so three live, themeable, now-patchable keys reach no pixel. They are
included in the preset anyway (leaving three of six without one recreates exactly
the drift this quest is about, and they cost nothing), and `docs/features/
interface.md` now says so in a parenthesis rather than implying all six are seen.
Whether to revive them as glyphs or retire the keys is a decision for someone
else; nothing was changed in the picker.

GLYPH CHOICE RE-EXAMINED AND UPHELD, 2026-08-30. The user asked whether the hint badge's Nerd Font lightbulb could be `nf-fa-lightbulb`, weighed it, and decided: **keep the Material Design pair.** No change.

WHAT THE LOOKUP FOUND, kept because it cost a scan and may be wanted later:

- `nf-fa-lightbulb` (the cheat-sheet name) is **`fa-lightbulb_o` in the font's own
  table, U+0F0EB**, present in all nine installed patched families. The names differ
  — which is exactly why SQ-1045 requires the codepoint be read from the font's
  `post`/`cmap` rather than a reference.
- **Font Awesome carries only ONE bulb.** There is no filled/outline pair in that
  family; `fa-lightbulb_o` is the whole of it.

WHY THAT SETTLED IT. `HintBadge` has three states, and two of them draw:

    None      nothing drawn
    Available a matching InvisiClues can be downloaded  → md-lightbulb_outline (hollow)
    Present   a hint file is on disk                    → md-lightbulb         (filled)

So the MD pair carries *available vs present* as a SHAPE change. Font Awesome could
only carry it as a colour change — the `#`/`M` pattern from SQ-1148 — and the user
chose the shape distinction over the Font Awesome silhouette. Consistent with the
house rule that a two-mode control changes shape wherever the font can express it,
and with SQ-1148's finding that colour-only is a DEGRADATION accepted when ASCII
offers no off-shape, not a preference.

Rejected alongside: pairing `fa-lightbulb_o` against `md-lightbulb`, which would
mix two icon families inside one control.

Nothing to implement. The shipped badges stand as SQ-1159 built them.
