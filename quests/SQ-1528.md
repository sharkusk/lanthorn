Full findings from today's verification pass, all against real captures the user added to machine-screenshots/ (currently untracked — commit them alongside the fix, they're the ground truth this bug is measured against):
- machine-screenshots/atari-sorcerer title.png — Claymorgue Castle's title card (bitmap family-C, index 99, file offset 0x4797 on "SAGA No. 13 - The Sorcerer of Claymorgue Castle _ side B.atr")
- machine-screenshots/atari-sorcerer game.png — Claymorgue Castle's room 1 (0x57BA on the same disk; NOTE 0x5617 is the RESERVED index-0 darkness card "IT'S TOO DARK!", not "room zero" — don't repeat that mixup)
- machine-screenshots/atari-scott colorbars.png, atari-scott-colorbars flicker 1/2.png — a pre-boot "Adjust TV" calibration card (RED/YELLOW/BLUE/GREEN bars), the same genre as the C64 Hulk's B01250R that the project already decided never to draw — not useful as an in-game reference, but the BROWN bar shows a visible checkerboard dither pattern even in one static frame, direct confirmation the pattern-fill mechanism is real
- machine-screenshots/atari-scott-adventurland title flicker1/2.png — Adventureland's own title card (line-art, index 99), captured as BOTH flickered phases (confirmed genuinely different content between the two frames, validating the two-palette 60Hz page-flip SQ-1525's disassembly found)

## Confirmed correct
- Every specimen decodes to the right picture — same layout, same composition, nothing missing or misplaced, on both the bitmap and line-art decoders.
- The checkerboard dither pattern is real (colorbars BROWN bar).
- The two-phase flicker is real and the two captured Adventureture frames genuinely differ region-by-region, matching the disassembly exactly (the "AI" box and the "Adventureland" banner background both flip between green/olive and blue across the two frames).

## Confirmed wrong, three independent instances of the same pattern
1. Claymorgue title text: right hue family throughout (blue/teal/yellow-green all distinct and correctly ordered) but every sampled value ran 15-35 RGB units off from the real capture — real #4E8AD5/#5DCB88/#C6FD74 vs lanthorn's #407BF0/#4DCFAC/#A2FF72 for the same three text regions. Consistent direction: lanthorn renders more saturated than the real machine.
2. Claymorgue room 1 (the castle exterior): a real hue-family error, not just imprecision. The castle body renders olive-green in lanthorn, gold-brown on real hardware. Sky and water share one lanthorn colour where the real capture clearly separates them; same for trees and grass. Record's stored colour bytes: [0x36, 0x87, 0x0E, 0x00] → lanthorn's atari_colour resolves them to #AD5F64 (dusty rose)/#5156FE (blue)/#E0E0E0 (grey) for values 1/2/3 — none of which is gold-brown, yet the real castle body is unmistakably gold-brown.
3. Adventureland title (line-art, so this is the SEPARATE PALETTE_A/PALETTE_B-pair-averaging path SQ-1525 built, not atari_colour called directly the same way — but atari_colour is still the underlying byte→RGB conversion feeding it): the globe renders bright pink-magenta in lanthorn versus a muted dark maroon-red on BOTH real flicker frames — a clear hue mismatch, not shading. The "Adventureland" banner background renders solid purple in lanthorn; the real frames alternate olive-green and blue, and a fair blend of those two should land on a muted teal-grey, not purple.

## The pattern worth investigating first
Text/line colours come out hue-correct with moderate imprecision (~15-35 RGB units). FILLED/PAINTED regions are the ones landing on a genuinely wrong hue family. This suggests filled regions may be resolving through a different colour-byte mapping than lines do (possibly a different pixel-value-to-colour-byte-slot assignment for fills specifically, or a compositing/blend-order issue), but this is an unconfirmed hypothesis, not yet verified — check it directly rather than assuming.

## What would help most, worth trying to locate before assuming more captures are needed
The "Adjust TV" colorbar cards (both the Claymorgue 4-bar and Adventureland 6-bar versions) are NOT in the picture-index table SQ-1524 built (same as the C64 case — a pre-boot-only calibration image, not part of the game's own picture data reachable through read_picture_table/read_line_art_table). If it can be located on the disk by raw scanning (the same method that found the darkness card and other special records earlier this session), it would give clean, NAMED, solid ground-truth colours (RED/YELLOW/BLUE/GREEN or WHITE/BROWN/ORANGE/YELLOW/BLUE/GREEN) — much better calibration data than reading colours off a busy dithered scene. Worth attempting before concluding more user-supplied captures are needed.

## Method notes for whoever picks this up
- `atari_colour` (crates/scott/src/saga_pictures.rs) is the single function both formats' colour resolution ultimately goes through — start there, but confirm the fills-vs-lines hypothesis with real decoded records before assuming the fix is a formula tweak alone.
- PHASE_DEGREES (-30.0) and SATURATION (0.30) are the two tunable constants; the doc comment already admits PHASE_DEGREES was picked only to match the hardware manual's hue-NAMING order, not to match exact RGB — first real numeric ground truth for tuning it is what this quest's screenshots provide.
- Falsify by the usual convention: revert whatever fix lands, confirm the specimen-based test fails with the exact originally-observed wrong colour, restore.
- The scratch verification scripts used to produce these findings were NOT committed (per the hard rule on scratch files) — the methodology is recorded here instead: decode a real record via the production readers (`scott::saga_atari::record_at`/`decode_record` for bitmap, `read_line_art_table`/`draw_line_art_record` for line-art), dump to a PPM, convert to PNG via `sips`, and compare against a precisely-cropped real capture (crop to the detected non-black picture bounding box — exclude the text-prompt area below it — then resize to the decoder's own canvas size for a fair pixel-for-pixel comparison; naive fractional-coordinate guessing without this alignment step produced garbage samples, learned the hard way this session).
