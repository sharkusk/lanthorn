## The finding

SQ-1128's corpus survey (60 stories with a readable Z grammar) turned up `bow`
as a verb in **twelve** — Sherlock, Trinity, Plundered Hearts, Beyond Zork,
Arthur, Shogun, Moonmist, Wishbringer ×2, Leather Goddesses ×2, Bureaucracy.

Every one of them had `bow` hidden from the verb column, because the band tests
quick-row membership through `mapper::direction::parse_direction`, and
`parse_direction("bow") == North` — a ship's bow points forward
(`mapper/src/direction.rs:43`). So a **mapper fact** is being reused as a
**band word-equivalence**, and the two questions are not the same one.

SQ-1128's new rule (a quick word only leaves the column if it cannot take an
object) returns `bow` automatically, since it takes one in all twelve. **The
symptom is cured; the reuse is not.**

## Why it is still worth fixing

The equivalence answers "is this word another spelling of a compass direction?"
for a mapper that must understand `GO BOW` on a ship. The band is asking
something else: "is this word already one click away on the quick row?" Those
coincide for `n`/`north` and diverge for every alias the mapper carries for
navigation rather than for vocabulary.

`stern`, `port` and `starboard` never appear as verbs anywhere in this corpus, so
**nothing else is currently hidden by it** — which is exactly why this is latent
rather than urgent, and why it will resurface as a puzzle when some future story
has a `port` verb.

## The fix

Give the band its own equivalence — quick-row membership by the word the story
actually holds, or by the command the quick button dispatches — rather than
borrowing the mapper's navigational aliasing. Then `parse_direction` goes back
to being about directions only.

Check while you are there whether anything else reuses `parse_direction` as a
vocabulary test rather than as a movement parser; that reuse is the actual
defect and `bow` is only the instance that surfaced.
