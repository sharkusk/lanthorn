## The limit, and where it is written down

`blorb::bitmap_font::Glyph`:

    /// One glyph: its advance width in pixels, and one byte per row.
    ///
    /// Only widths up to 8 are representable, which covers every font measured
    /// here — the Amiga's are at most 8 and the Macintosh's are 7. A parser
    /// refuses a font needing more rather than silently truncating a glyph.
    pub rows: Vec<u8>,

Enforced at `mac_font.rs:79` (`rect_w > 8` rejects the whole font) and `:109`
(`img_w + bearing > 8` per glyph), and at `amiga_font.rs` (`if gw > 8 { return
None }`).

Refusing rather than truncating was the right call. The ASSUMPTION behind the
bound — "covers every font measured here" — is the part that has expired, and it
expired the moment we pointed the readers at system media. Same shape as SQ-0917's
`ch >= 16` face-selection floor: a limit that was true of its inputs when written
and silently wrong when the inputs widened.

## Measured on the user's own disks, 2026-08-23

**`~/.lanthorn/MacOS_6.0.8_System_Startup.img`** — mounts clean as "System
Startup"; `System Folder/System` carries a 500,309-byte resource fork with the
whole font suite. Every id is present and almost nothing parses:

    FONT 393/394/396/398/402/404/408  Geneva 9,10,12,14,18,20,24   ALL refused
    FONT 2698/2700                    Helvetica 10, 12             refused
    FONT 2570/2572                    Times 10, 12                 refused
    FONT 2954/2956                    Symbol 10, 12                refused
    FONT 12                           Chicago 12                   refused
    FONT 2826                         Courier 10   -> 8x11  parses (monospaced)
    FONT 521                          Monaco 9     -> 6x11  parses (monospaced)
    FONT 524                          Monaco 12    -> 7x15  parses (monospaced)

**Geneva 12 is FONT 396 and it is right there** — the face SQ-1036 says the
Macintosh actually painted, at Apple's own advances. We cannot read it.

**`~/.lanthorn/Workbench v1.2 rev 33.56 (1987)…adf`** — a full `fonts/` drawer,
same result:

    fonts/ruby/8,12,15 · opal/9,12 · sapphire/14,19 · diamond/12,20 ·
    garnet/9,16 · emerald/17,20        ALL refused
    fonts/topaz/11  -> 8x11 baseline=8 prop=false lo=32   parses

The `*.font` files (264-784 B) are AmigaDOS descriptor files, not bitmaps, and are
correctly rejected — that part is right.

**One line separates the two lists: the ones that parse are the MONOSPACED ones.**
topaz, Monaco and Courier are 8, 7 and 8 wide. Every proportional face on either
disk has glyphs past 8 px and is refused wholesale.

Arthur's `char.data` hid this: it is proportional but its widest glyph is exactly
8, so it slipped under the bound and the limit stayed invisible.

## What the fix has to do

Widen the row representation past 8 columns and drop the guards that reject on it.
`Vec<u8>` per row becomes something that can hold a real glyph — a `u16` row, a
bit-slice, or bytes-per-row derived from the font rectangle. Note:

* **`Glyph::width` is the ADVANCE and is already a `u8`** — fine up to 255, no
  change needed there. It is `rows` that is the constraint.
* Both parsers pre-shift a glyph by its left bearing into the row
  (`mac_font`'s offset/width table, `amiga_font`'s `tf_CharKern`), so the row must
  hold bearing + ink, not just ink.
* The renderer reads rows MSB-leftmost in `bitfont::blit_glyph_styled` and in
  `blit_metric_glyph`; widening the row type touches both, plus `vga16` and
  `misc7x14` which pack 8-wide rows and should keep working unchanged.
* Keep the refuse-don't-truncate discipline at whatever the new bound is. A font
  wider than the new representation must still be declined rather than clipped.

## Why this is worth doing now

It is the prerequisite for SQ-1037 (system fonts off a user-supplied disk) and
therefore for SQ-1036 (the Macintosh's real body face). Both are blocked on it
outright — the disks are in place and correct, and this is the only thing between
them and Geneva 12.

It is also pre-release, so widening a public type in `blorb` costs nothing today.

Related: SQ-1037, SQ-1036, SQ-1009 (the pen that will draw these), SQ-0916 (where
the 8-wide assumption was first written down).
