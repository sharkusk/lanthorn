## The gap

SQ-1041's `vocab::stems()` handles regular endings — `opening` → `open`,
`lighting` → `light`. English irregulars are unreachable by any suffix rule:

| typed | wanted |
|---|---|
| `lit` | `light` |
| `took` | `take` |
| `went` | `go` |
| `caught` | `catch` |
| `broke` | `break` |

The lane did the right thing rather than papering over it: a test asserts
`stems("lit")` is **empty**, so the limitation is pinned rather than hidden
behind a rule that half-works.

## Why it matters more than it looks

A player who types `lit lamp` is not making a typo — they are speaking English.
The one-keystroke near-miss rule cannot help (`lit` to `light` is two edits and
the rule stops at one, deliberately, because distance two is what turns `marcus`
into `march`). So this is a silent miss with no fallback.

## The fix, and where it belongs

An irregular verb table — a few hundred pairs, closed-class and stable since
Middle English, so it does not rot. Map inflection to lemma, consult it when
`stems()` returns nothing.

**Do this alongside or after [[SQ-1110]]**, not before. That quest is already
establishing lemma handling (its table is lemmas-only and its consumer must
lemmatize before lookup), and it is acquiring a licensed lexical source that may
carry the irregular forms already — WordNet ships an exception list
(`verb.exc`) that is exactly this data. Building a hand-written table here first
would duplicate it and then have to be reconciled.

So the shape is likely: SQ-1110 extracts the exception list as a by-product of
work it is doing anyway, and this quest wires it into `vocab::stems()`.

## Do not widen the near-miss distance instead

Raising the edit-distance threshold to two would catch `lit` and would also
catch a great deal of nonsense — SQ-1041 chose one keystroke deliberately and
its silence cases depend on it. Fix the morphology, not the tolerance.

## What shipped, and the one thing it does NOT reach

`crates/verb-synonyms/src/irregular_forms.tsv` — WordNet 3.0's `verb.exc` and
`noun.exc`, generated by a new `verb-synonyms-gen irregulars` subcommand.
**2,417 verb rows, 2,107 noun rows, 4,520 lines after de-duplication, 4,424
distinct forms, 78 KB.** `verb_synonyms::irregular_bases` reads it lazily beside
the synonym groups; `vocab::stems` appends its answers to the suffix rule's, for
every word rather than only on a miss.

Nouns as well as verbs, deliberately: `stems` serves every position in a
command, so `mice → mouse` is `lit → light` one slot to the right.

The lookup returns a SLICE because WordNet puts two bases on 26 verb lines and
72 noun lines and they are not all spelling variants — `axes` is `ax` AND
`axis`, `singing` is `sing` AND `singe`, `overflown` is `overflow` AND
`overfly`. (Worth noting: `axes` is NOT the verb-vs-noun case the brief assumed;
WordNet lists it under nouns alone, with both bases on one line.)

### The finding: `lit` is three letters, and `MIN_LEN` is four

The quest's headline example does not fire, and not because of the table.
`StoryVocabulary::offer` refuses anything whose truncated spelling is under
`MIN_LEN` (4) before any source runs, so `lit lamp` at Zork I is silent even
though `irregular_bases("lit") == ["light"]` and Zork holds `light`. That gate
is older than both word sources and is already applied to an exact table hit:
`a_plainly_spelled_story` pins `don` → `wear` as refused, commented "the gate is
older than this source and is not relaxed for it."

Relaxing it for exact lookups (as opposed to near misses, which is the
reasoning MIN_LEN was written from) is a real design question, and it reverses a
decision recorded in a pinned test — so it was left alone rather than decided in
this lane. **`lit`, `ate`, `saw`, `won`, `got` and every other three-letter
irregular are out of reach until that is settled.** The four-letter-and-up ones
work: on Zork I r88, `took lamp` → `take`, `broke lamp` → `break`, `caught rope`
→ `catch`, all pinned in `vocabulary_offer.rs`, all falsified by reverting the
loop (`took` then offers `look`, the near miss).
