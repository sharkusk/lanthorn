## The evidence, from two lanes that did not know about each other

`zvm::grammar::decode_roles` reads `GrammarFormat::InfocomV6` using **Inform's** flag layout — `$01` verb, `$80` noun. Infocom's V6 titles do not keep them there.

**SQ-1135's measurement**, taken through real sessions rather than by reading bytes:

| story | what the noun bit selects |
|---|---|
| Arthur r74 | `are is was were will` — and **not** `crystal`, `torque`, `sword` |
| Zork Zero r393 | `a all and of the then` |
| Shogun r322 | nothing at all |

Every one of those is wrong, and wrong in a different way, which is what makes the layout unguessable from one title. Arthur's own bytes fit `$01`/`$02`/`$04`/`$40` neatly — and Zork Zero and Shogun both falsify that reading, so a lane that had only looked at Arthur would have shipped a confident wrong answer.

**SQ-1120's independent finding**, from the adjectives work: `decode_roles` does not decode `adjective` for the V6 layout **at all**. That lane also measured the DESC bit on the three V6 titles — Zork Zero 11 of 1624 words, Shogun 17 of 1389, Arthur 9 of 1059 — against a fifth to a third of the dictionary on every pre-V6 Infocom story. Those numbers are not a sparse adjective list; they are a bit that means something else here.

Two lanes arriving at the same conclusion from opposite directions is the strongest evidence in the quest.

## Why it has not bitten harder

Nothing user-facing depended on V6 roles until now, and both lanes routed around rather than through:

* SQ-1120 gated its adjective detection on **version >= 4** and reads a property rather than the flag byte.
* SQ-1135 added `Introspect::all_object_words` and asks the OBJECTS which words file under them, instead of asking the dictionary what kind of word it is. Glulx and Scott keep the role-bit filter; only zvm takes the new route.

So this is currently a wrong answer nobody is asking. That will not stay true — `WordRoles` is public API on a crate whose stated priority is to be embeddable outside lanthorn, and an embedder has no way to know the V6 answers are fiction.

## What solving it needs

**Do not guess from one title.** That is the trap this quest exists to record: Arthur alone supports a clean, wrong hypothesis. Any candidate layout must be checked against all three V6 titles in `stories/` — `arthur-r74-s890714.z6`, `zork0-r393-s890714.z6`, `shogun-r322-s890706.z6` — and predict all three.

**The parser is the oracle**, as SQ-1118 and SQ-1120 both established: a proposed noun bit is right when `zvm-cli` accepts the words it selects in a noun position and rejects the ones it does not. A reader verified only against itself proves nothing, and this is precisely the defect class that produced the wrong answer in the first place.

**A useful cross-check already exists**: SQ-1135's `all_object_words` gives, per V6 title, the set of words the game itself files under objects. A correct noun bit should agree with that set closely. Disagreement in either direction is diagnostic rather than fatal — Arthur's `password` object rewrites its own parse names mid-puzzle — but it is a far stronger signal than counting words.

**Check whether V6 is even one layout.** Three titles from one publisher across 1988–89 is a small sample, and Zork Zero selecting function words while Shogun selects nothing may mean two different things rather than one bug.

Related: [[SQ-1120]] shipped the V4+ adjectives and holds the DESC census; [[SQ-1135]] holds the noun-bit measurement and the `all_object_words` seam that routes around it.

## Settled — the layout is $01 verb / $02 noun / $04 adjective, in the LAST byte, and nothing above bit 2 is shared

Fixed on branch `worktree-agent-af84515ffcb57dd4e` (commit `3cd72ef9`), with the games' own parsers as the oracle.

### Where the flag byte is

The dictionary entry is `[6-byte key][2-byte verb-record address][flags…]`. The flags are the byte(s) AFTER the address, so the flag byte is the LAST byte of the entry — which is where `scan_dictionary` was already reading it. Zork Zero's entry is 9 bytes (one flag byte), Arthur's and Shogun's are 10 (two). The verb records sit immediately below the dictionary in every case (Arthur $43xx–$47xx under a dictionary at $4a5d; Zork Zero $74xx–$79xx under $7cc9; Shogun $53xx–$5bxx under $6023).

### The three bits that carry

| bit | meaning |
|---|---|
| `$01` | verb (already used by `verb_key`/`load_v6`, and already validated against `infodump -g`) |
| `$02` | **noun** |
| `$04` | **adjective** (ZIL's DESC) |

### Why V6 is one layout for three bits and three layouts above them

Line the flag field up as one big-endian bitfield (Arthur/Shogun's penult byte is the high half) and the classes are allocated one bit per declared part of speech, from bit 0 up, rounded to whole bytes — each game's own ZIL declaration order:

| bit | Zork Zero (1 byte) | Arthur (2 bytes) | Shogun (2 bytes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | verb | verb | verb |
| 1 | noun | noun | noun |
| 2 | adjective | adjective | adjective |
| 3 | directions + `once/twice/thrice` | `don't once thrice twice` | articles |
| 4 | prepositions + articles | `a all an any either` | `again be not oops` |
| 5 | `' again but except the` | `' again be not of the` | **DIRECTIONS** |
| 6 | `. ! ? ask order tell then` | **DIRECTIONS** | `am are is was were` |
| 7 | buzzword aggregate | `am are is shall was were will` | `how what when where who why` |
| 8–15 | (no second byte) | wh-words, modals, `, and`, PREPOSITIONS, `but except`, `ask order tell`, `"`, terminators | modals, PREPOSITIONS, `but except`, `ask order tell`, `and ,`, `'`, terminators |

Shogun is Arthur's allocation **minus one class** with everything above it shifted down a bit. So `preposition` and `special` are left FALSE for V6 — `WordRoles::from_raw`'s documented "this family has no such bit" — rather than naming whichever bit fits the title in front of you. That is the trap this quest recorded, confirmed: Arthur's `$40` really is a clean direction bit, and Shogun's `$40` is `am are is shall was were will`.

### The oracle, and it is decisive

Each story booted headless (`Machine::new` + `init_caps` + `set_screen_dims`; **`init_caps` is load-bearing** — without it Zork Zero's read buffer carries max-length 0, every `supply_line` writes nothing, and every probe silently measures an empty line), driven to its first prompt (Arthur answering `N` to the restore question), snapshotted with Quetzal, then `x <word>` from that snapshot for every ≥3-letter all-alphabetic non-verb dictionary word:

| story | `$02` named a thing | `$02` refused | `$04`-only got "…OBJECT right here" | `$04`-only named a thing | no-bit named a thing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arthur r74 | 381 / 502 | 0 | 111 / 126 | 0 | **0** of 113 |
| Zork Zero r393 | 621 / 751 | 0 | 240 / 286 | 0 | **0** of 111 |
| Shogun r322 | 408 / 555 | 2 | 132 / 155 | 0 | **0** of 95 |

The parser distinguishes the two classes in its own words: a noun gets "You can't see any **spyglass** right here", an adjective standing alone gets "You can't see any **elongated object** right here". Shogun's two refused nouns are named in the test: `the` (wants a noun after it) and `gonzale`.

Under the old reading the same sweep gives Arthur 5 nouns, Zork Zero 11, Shogun **0** — and 381/620/408 role-less words that the parsers take as names of things. All five new cases fail that way when the fix is reverted.

### Cross-checks

* `ParseNames::all` (SQ-1135's seam): every one of the ~1,700 object `SYNONYM` words across the three stories carries `$02`. The adjective half is total on Arthur (352 slots) and Shogun (410) and has exactly four exceptions on Zork Zero (685 slots): `huge`, `mighty`, `smaller`, `tiny`, all `$00`. Not a hole in the DESC bit — Zork Zero rewrites a size word to its canonical spelling before the dictionary is consulted (`x big`, also `$00`, is answered "you can't see any **large** object right here"), so the alias's flag byte is genuinely empty while the property still lists it. Named in the test rather than absorbed by a ratio.
* The V6 grammar TABLES are byte-identical before and after for all three stories, so `load_v6`'s use of `roles.noun` to bound the verb-record area is unaffected and `grammar_tables.rs`'s infodump agreement still holds.
* Arthur's `was` is `$84` — a descriptor as well as a be-verb — so it is the one of the seven `$80` words the parser answers rather than refuses. Still not a noun.

### Landed

* `crates/zvm/src/grammar.rs` — `F_INFOCOM_V6_VERB/_NOUN/_DESC`, the measurement written up beside them, `decode_roles` and `scan_dictionary`'s V6 comment.
* `crates/zvm/tests/v6_word_roles.rs` — new, five cases, skips vacuously without `stories/`, each with a non-vacuity floor.
* `crates/app/tests/suites/command_band.rs` — the case that deliberately pinned the wrong answer ("the day someone reads the V6 flag byte correctly this case says what changed") now pins the right one and is renamed. **SQ-1135's `all_object_words` seam is untouched** — it is what the band reads, and only a word array can follow Arthur's `password` object rewriting its own parse names mid-puzzle.
* `docs/features/interface.md` — the paragraph claiming the V6 bits are undecoded.

Gate: `cargo nextest run --workspace` → 6720 enumerated, 6720 run, 6720 passed, grep gate prints 0. `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings` clean.

### Left open deliberately

`WordRoles::preposition`'s doc still says "Infocom's PREP bit ($08)", which is true of V1–5 and inapplicable to V6. `grammar-model` is shared with `gvm` and was outside this lane's scope. A V6 preposition set IS derivable — from the story's own syntax lines rather than the flag byte — if anyone ever wants one.
