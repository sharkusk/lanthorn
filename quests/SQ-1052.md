## What the capture already settles

* **The runs are there.** `win1` is a one-row 581x16 strip at (29,193) carrying **122 paint runs** — Arthur emits its bar one character per run, so 122 is a full bar, not a stub. The model publishes it as `Grid — 122 run(s)`. Nothing is missing upstream of the renderer.
* **It is a 7x15 cell**, i.e. the Macintosh (SQ-0917), and `usable 581px = 83 cols`. `581 / 7 = 83`, so the declared arithmetic is consistent.
* **The window is 16px tall against a 15px cell.** One row, with a pixel over.
* The same runs render correctly on the F2/F3/F4/F5 screens, per the report — so this is the score-bar FRAME, not the runs or the face.

## Where to look

The neighbours matter here. SQ-1035 fixed the hybrid twin of a score-bar defect on Arthur's AMIGA press, and SQ-1051 fixed raster prose losing its tails on this same MACINTOSH press to a character cap standing in for a pixel width. "Only the location shows, reversed, and the rest is blank" is the shape a REVERSE-ROW FLOOD makes when it wins the row: `v6_layout::fill_reverse_row_gaps` fills the gaps around a pure reverse-video row, and `render::screen::row_is_reverse_bar` decides whether a row counts as a band at all. Arthur's bar IS all-reversed and DOES carry text, so it should flood and then be re-stamped — check whether the glyphs are being painted and then covered, rather than never painted.

Note the 16px window against the 15px cell as a candidate off-by-one: a row test written as `>= cell.h` or a rect clipped to `y_size` could take the strip one way and the glyph loop the other.

Reproduce on `stories/Arthur Quest 4 Excalibur.2mg` or whichever Macintosh Arthur medium the user is on — name the release and serial in any finding, and state the turn count, since Arthur's ProDOS press renders identically at 6 and 40 keypresses (SQ-0883).
