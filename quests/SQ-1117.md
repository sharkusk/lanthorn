## The point

`zvm::grammar`, `gvm::grammar`, `grammar-model` and `zvm::dictionary` now answer
authoritatively what a story knows. Four places in `app` still answer it by
guessing, each having grown its own heuristic before the readers existed:

| consumer | what it uses now | what it should read |
|---|---|---|
| command band, VERB column | `BUILTIN_VERBS`, 36 hardcoded verbs | the story's verb table |
| command band, verb shapes | `Arity` (Solo/Object/ObjectOpt/Pair) + `prep` | `SyntaxLine` — which supports several lines per verb, as `Arity` cannot |
| Tab completion + band `here` fallback | `room_words_from_text`: ~40 stop words, bespoke splitter, 3-char floor | `Dictionary::tokenise` + `lookup` |
| band object columns | a word guessed from the object's **short name** | the parse-name property (prop 1) — still missing |
| guidance (SQ-1041) | the readers, correctly | — already right |

Guidance is the only consumer already reading the real thing. That is the shape
everything else should converge on.

**These are not four bugs, they are one missing seam.** Each consumer
independently reconstructs "what does this story know, and what is in scope right
now" — which is exactly the pattern CLAUDE.md's refactoring policy names: facts
that must be considered together, reassembled separately in several places, each
plausible on its own and quietly divergent. `Arity` cannot express a verb with two
syntax lines; the 3-char floor drops `x`, `n`, `up`, `in`; the stop list overrules
a story that genuinely implements `here`. Every one of those is self-consistent
and wrong.

## What to build

**One vocabulary view, per story, refreshed per turn**, behind the `Engine` seam
so Glk never leaks and all three engines answer:

- the verbs this story knows, with their real syntax lines;
- every dictionary word and its roles;
- what is in scope now, and **the words that refer to it** (not what it is
  printed as).

`Engine::knows_word` (SQ-1041) is the first method of this seam and the proof it
works. Grow it rather than starting again.

## Supersede, do not accumulate

The user's instruction is that the new work **replaces** the old, not that it
sits beside it. A heuristic left in as a second path is one that will be reached
by something, drift, and disagree.

**But keep the fallbacks that are honest ones**, and follow the pattern already
in the tree: `command_band.rs:312` reads the object tree when
`session.introspect()` answers and scrapes only when it does not, setting
`here_is_seen` so the column **labels itself "seen"** rather than passing a guess
off as fact. That is the model. Authoritative source primary; heuristic only
where the reader genuinely cannot answer — a story whose grammar will not parse
(SQ-1101's non-Inform files, a Glulx chain that will not close), or an engine
with no object tree — and always labelled as the lesser thing.

`BUILTIN_VERBS` therefore survives as a **fallback**, and stops being the source.

## Sequencing

The one genuinely missing piece is the **parse-name reader** (object property 1),
which nothing exposes today. It gates the scope half and is wanted by three
quests, so build it once, in `zvm`/`gvm`, not in `app`.

Children, all now consumers of this rather than independent fixes:

- [[SQ-1111]] — the VERB column and retiring `Arity`.
- [[SQ-1116]] — the noun extractor and its stop-word list.
- [[SQ-1042]] — scope-aware completion; needs the parse-name reader.
- [[SQ-1107]] — momentary reveal; needs the same reader for its strongest tier.

Do the seam and the parse-name reader first; the four then become small.

## Progress, and one row made concrete (2026-08-28)

Three of the five rows in the table above are now done:

- **VERB column** — SQ-1111. `BUILTIN_VERBS` demoted to a labelled fallback;
  Zork I went from 23 generic verbs to 248 of its own spellings.
- **Verb shapes** — SQ-1111. `Arity` deleted; `VerbLine` derives from
  `SyntaxLine`, so a verb with several syntax lines is representable at last.
- **Candidate words** — SQ-1116. The stop list, the bespoke splitter and the
  3-character floor are gone; `Engine::split_like_parser` lends the story's own
  tokeniser. Zork I went from 51 candidates (37 of which the parser rejects) to
  26, every one accepted.
- **Object words** — SQ-1118 built the reader (`ObjectWords`, all three
  engines). Not yet wired into the band.

## The remaining row, now precise

**The band's object columns still guess from the printed short name**, and
SQ-1116 sharpened what is needed. Its scrape now correctly stops inventing words
— but on Glulx, where the scrape is the *only* completion source, the column
shows the story's **function words** (`the`, `a`, `you`, `my`) because they
genuinely are dictionary words. The stop list used to hide them by accident,
while also hiding ~70 invented ones.

The right filter is the story's own **noun role**, not an English list.
`StoryVocabulary` already holds the roles and **exposes no public accessor for
them** — that is the one-line gap. Adding it means editing `vocab.rs`, which
SQ-1116's brief put off-limits because another lane held it.

So this row is now: expose the roles, filter the object columns by noun, and
wire `ObjectWords` in so the columns offer what a thing can be **called** rather
than a word guessed from what it is **printed** as. That is [[SQ-1042]]'s job and
it is no longer blocked on anything.
