## The problem this exists to solve, stated plainly

A player types `illuminate lamp`. The story wants `light`. To suggest `light`
you must know what `illuminate` MEANS — and nothing in the story file records
that.

Every mechanism available today operates on **form**, and all of them fail here:

| mechanism | on `illuminate` -> `light` |
|---|---|
| edit distance | distance 8 on a 10-letter word — ranks near last |
| stemming / morphology | `illuminat-` reaches nothing |
| grammar shape (`SyntaxLine::accepts`) | matches on noun COUNT and prepositions, not on which object — `illuminate lamp` narrows to "verbs with a one-noun line", i.e. most verbs |
| the story's own synonyms (`Verb.words`) | only groups words the story ALREADY knows; `illuminate` is not one of them |

So the bridge has to come from outside the story file. There is no third option,
and SQ-1041 cannot solve this case no matter how its ranking is written.

## The shape: invert the thesaurus, generate offline, ship the table

Do NOT ship a general English thesaurus and query it at runtime. Instead:

1. The IF verb vocabulary is **bounded** — a few thousand distinct verbs across
   the whole corpus, with ~100 standard actions doing most of the work. Harvest
   it: `Grammar::verb_words()` over every story in `stories/` and `unit_tests/`
   gives the real set, on all three engines.
2. Expand each of those verbs through WordNet (or Moby) **offline**.
3. Ship the resulting **reverse map**: player word -> IF verbs it could mean.

Anything outside IF's vocabulary never enters the table, because there is nothing
for it to map to. That buys the coverage of a full thesaurus at a fraction of the
size, with no runtime dependency, no network, and an artifact a human can read
and check — `illuminate -> light, burn` is either in it or it is not.

**Ship the generator with the table.** This is the persistence rule from
CLAUDE.md applied to a build artifact: nothing goes in without its regeneration
inputs, or the table becomes a blob nobody can reproduce when the corpus or the
source changes.

## The invariant that keeps it honest

Expand the player's word through the table, then **intersect with THIS story's
actual dictionary**, and display only what survives. The table proposes; the
story disposes. The player is never shown a word the parser would reject, so
this does not violate SQ-1041's rule that lanthorn offers the story's own
vocabulary and never rewrites what the player typed — the table only decides
WHICH of the story's words to surface.

## Licence — settle before any data lands in the tree

WordNet 3.0's licence is permissive and attribution-style and should sit fine
under lanthorn's BSD-3-Clause; Moby Thesaurus is public domain and is the
fallback if it does not. **Read the actual licence text rather than trusting
that summary**, and record the finding in the quest before committing any
derived data. Attribution, if required, goes wherever the third-party notices
live.

## Placement

`app`, or a small crate below it — NOT `zvm`/`gvm`/`scott`, which take zero
external dependencies and have no business knowing about English. `app` already
carries `regex`, `zip`, `serde`, `clap`.

## Relationship to the other quests

- **SQ-1041** ships first and without this: typos (edit distance genuinely works
  for `lanturn` -> `lantern`), morphology, and the story's own verb synonyms via
  `Verb.words`. Its ranking should have a seam this plugs into later.
- A browsable "what verbs does this story understand" is the fallback that needs
  no semantics at all and is filed separately — when nothing matches, showing the
  player the vocabulary beats a worse guess, and a human matches meaning far
  better than any heuristic we can ship.

## Lookup cost, and the one ordering rule that keeps it free

**Search is not a performance concern, provided the two sets are searched
differently.** Recorded here because the wiring happens in a later quest and this
is where whoever does it will look.

| step | cost |
|---|---|
| exact lookup in the synonym table | O(1) hash hit — table size irrelevant |
| building the map (first use, lazy via `OnceLock`) | single-digit ms, once per session, often never |
| edit distance vs THIS STORY's dictionary (hundreds to a few thousand words) | well under a millisecond |
| edit distance vs the synonym table's keys (tens of thousands) | 10–30ms, and pointless |

**The rule: fuzzy against the small set, exact against the big one.**
Typo-match the player's word against the story's own dictionary; only ever do
**exact** lookups into the synonym table. Never scan the table's keys fuzzily.

That is not only faster, it is more precise. A near-miss against a word this
story genuinely knows (`lanturn` → `lantern`) is strong evidence. A near-miss
against a generic English table is noise, and would let a typo of a synonym of a
verb this story does not have produce a suggestion — two inferences deep, with
nothing anchoring either.

**Timing context:** the lookup fires between the player's command and the game's
reply, after the parser has already failed. The Z-machine turn that produced that
failure took longer than the whole lookup will. There is no budget pressure here,
so do not trade auditability for speed — see the storage note above, which
settles on a greppable TSV for exactly that reason.
