## The research, carried over from SQ-1120 in full

Nothing below is implemented. The lane's measuring tool was a scratch harness and was deleted with the worktree, so this note IS the record — it is complete enough to rebuild in one sitting. Do not go looking for the code.

### There is no compiled-in constant to chase — SQ-1118's premise was wrong

The one-byte adjective numbers resolve against the **dictionary itself**, with nothing external involved. An adjective's dictionary entry carries `DESC` ($20) in its flags byte with `DATA_FIRST` = `ADJ_FIRST` ($02), and **its own number is its first data byte**. Zork I r88's `brass` reads `flags=$22 d0=$dd`; the brass lantern's property 16 is the single byte `$dd`.

Building `number → word` is one pass over `zvm::grammar::dictionary_words`, reading `mem.read_byte(w.address + 4)` and `+ 5`. It is **total** on every V3 title measured — every DESC-with-ADJ_FIRST word has a number, from 92 of them in Zork III to 250 in Leather Goddesses.

So the open problem is only ever *which property holds the numbers*. The game's parser reaching it "through a compiled-in constant" is not something a reader must recover; it is something a reader must **detect**.

### The oracle: the object's own printed name

The adjective a story stores for a thing usually appears in what the story PRINTS for it — `brass lantern`, `small mailbox`, `Dirigible Hangar`. That is a free per-object cross-check the count-based ranking never used.

Method: build `number → word`; then for each object and each property whose data is 1–8 bytes that are ALL known adjective numbers, ask whether any adjective it names is a prefix of a word in that object's own `short_name`. Score each property by the fraction of its objects that agree.

### Result: 45–84% for the right property, at most 9% for any rival, across twenty V3 titles

Every title separated, no exception and no near miss. `nouns` is what `ParseNames` already detects; `adj?` is the property the oracle picks; `rate` its agreement; `next` the best rival's.

The three cases SQ-1118's refusal named as undecidable are all settled:

* **Zork I picks p16** — the property the disassembly names.
* **Hitchhiker picks p30 at 64% against p29's 2%.** The two properties that "pick different properties" under object-count and adjective-coverage are not close under this test.
* **Moonmist picks p17 at 77% against p18's 3% and p19's 2%.** The "coin toss" is a 25x margin.

**And the count-based ranking is actively WRONG**, which is exactly why the original attempt found no margin. Ballyhoo's largest byte-property is p9 on 177 objects at 0% agreement, while the adjectives are p10 on 108. Ranking by size picks the wrong property on several titles; ranking by printed-name agreement picks the right one on all of them.

## Four things to settle before shipping, with a view on each

1. **The threshold, stated as a RATIO not an absolute.** The corpus gap is a 45% floor against a 9% ceiling, so any absolute in 15–35% separates these 23 stories — but agreement depends on how chatty a game's short names are (Plundered Hearts at 45% is the sparse end), and an absolute will not travel. "The leader must beat the runner-up by N times" is the shape that will.
2. **Stories the oracle starves must REFUSE, not settle.** A game whose objects are mostly unnamed, or whose short names are all-caps room labels, gives the oracle nothing to agree with. Refusing is the established answer here — it is what SQ-1118 did, and `Adjectives::Unavailable` already exists to express it. That refusal wants its own test.
3. **The parser is the oracle, per game.** `take brass lantern → Taken.` on r88 confirms Zork I; each candidate wants one real command before its number is believed. A self-consistent reader verified only against itself is the failure mode this repo keeps logging.
4. **Shape has only been checked on V3.** All twenty titles here hold 1–8 bytes. Nothing is known about V1/V2 (no fixtures in `stories/`) or about a property mixing numbers with something else. Gate detection to where the evidence is.

## Why this is deferred rather than dispatched

The DESC-bit census that SQ-1120's second note asked for came back and undercut the case for urgency: **all 34 pre-V6 Infocom stories set the DESC bit**, a fifth to a third of each dictionary. So `vocab::typeable_name`'s fallback is safe on every V1-3 story in the corpus, and the three V6 titles that do NOT set it are covered by the V4+ property that shipped.

What remains is a hypothetical V1-3 story, outside this corpus, with no DESC bit. Real, narrow, and robustness rather than a defect — which is why this is worth keeping and not worth doing now. Doing it would delete a workaround and its documented assumption, which is the honest reason to pick it up later.
