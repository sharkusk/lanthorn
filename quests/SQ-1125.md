## Separate the two motivations — they point different ways

**Latency is the weak argument.** SQ-1121 measured 12 ms an offer on Zork I and
26 ms on Coloratura — below the threshold a human notices. Only
Counterfeit-Monkey-scale games are slow, and [[SQ-1124]]'s plan already handles
the tail: if the player has typed again the answer is stale and is dropped
silently. **Measure how often "late" actually happens before letting it drive a
permanent layout change.**

**Discoverability is the strong argument, and it has already bitten.** The user
ran the SQ-1045 branch, saw nothing, and reasonably concluded the feature was
broken — it is invisible until it fires, and on a default config nothing fired at
all. A persistent surface says *this exists and is watching*. Same gap
[[SQ-1123]]'s border toggles address, and the two should be weighed together
rather than solving it twice.

## What moving it would COST — this is the part to argue against

[[SQ-1045]] deliberately made the transcript the **record**:

- assists persist in scrollback, so a player can look back at what help they got;
- `export-transcript` writes `Lanthorn: ` onto exactly those lines
  (`assist::export_line`), so attribution survives a copy-paste into a bug
  report — that was the whole answer to dropping the on-screen text marker.

A panel is ephemeral. Move the assists there and the export marker has nothing
to mark, and the scrollback record disappears. If a panel is added, the
transcript should almost certainly **keep** the line and the panel be an
additional live surface — accepting the duplication deliberately rather than
discovering it.

## Two more constraints

- **The bottom dock is taken.** The command band (F2) already sits there. Two
  bottom panels on a 24-row terminal is a squeeze, and the band is itself
  optional/toggleable — so the layout question is really "what owns the bottom,
  and what happens when both are on".
- **The caution tone stays inline regardless.** *"Burning the leaflet cannot be
  undone"* belongs immediately before the consequence, in the flow of play.
  Moving the most urgent assist to a panel makes it the easiest to miss. If a
  panel happens, it takes the help tone only, and that split needs stating in
  `assist.rs` where the tones are defined.

## Recommendation to weigh, not a decision

Keep the transcript as the record. Consider the panel as an **additional** live
surface justified by discoverability, sized and scheduled alongside SQ-1123, and
only after SQ-1124 has measured how often an offer really does arrive late.

## Two better options (user, 2026-08-28), and why they beat the panel

1. **Ghost text at the prompt**, like a shell autosuggestion, hidden the moment
   the player types.
2. **Right-justified on the prompt line**, hidden when the player's input would
   overlap it.

**The insight neither of us started with: these solve the latency problem BY
CONSTRUCTION.** A panel or an inline transcript line is tied to the *turn*, so a
late offer needs staleness logic — has the player typed since? what do we do if
so? A prompt-anchored hint is tied to *prompt state*: if the offer arrives 300 ms
late and the prompt is still empty, it appears; if the player has started typing,
it is already hidden. [[SQ-1124]]'s stale-answer problem does not need solving,
it stops existing.

That is a stronger argument than anything in the panel's favour.

## Comparing the four

| surface | latency | layout cost | transcript record | conflicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| inline (today) | needs staleness logic | none | **kept** | — |
| panel below story | needs staleness logic | a pane, and the bottom dock is taken | lost unless duplicated | command band |
| ghost at cursor | solved by construction | none | lost unless duplicated | **Tab completion owns that space** |
| **right-justified on prompt** | solved by construction | none | — | none |

**Option 1's conflict is real, not cosmetic.** `state.suggestions` and Tab
completion already ghost at the cursor (`input::recompute_suggestions`). Two
things appearing in the same place for different reasons is a genuine collision.
Option 1's *timing* insight is still right and should be kept: the window where
the prompt is empty, right after a rejection, is exactly when the player is
deciding what to type.

## Recommendation

**Right-justified on the prompt line, with the transcript keeping the record.**

It reads as a status hint rather than a message, which is also what makes the
duplication comfortable: the transcript line is history, the prompt hint is a
nudge, and nobody reads them as the same thing said twice. `export-transcript`'s
`Lanthorn: ` marker and the scrollback record both survive untouched, so
[[SQ-1045]]'s design is not quietly undone.

## The one real cost: width

`try instead — light · turn on · burn` is ~40 columns. Right-justified on a
40-column pane there is nothing left. **State the degradation rather than
discovering it at render time**: first suggestion only, then nothing at all — and
never truncate mid-word into something the parser would reject, which would be
the same defect class as offering a word the story does not hold.

The caution tone still stays inline, per the note above — a nudge at the edge of
the prompt is the wrong weight for "this cannot be undone".
