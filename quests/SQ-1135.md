## The user's framing, which is the whole quest

> "the point of this is not to only show objects that have specific attributes. If
> anything that narrows things too much. Rather any objects/nouns the game 'knows'
> should be available to allow the user to explore as wide as possible."

The assist set exists to make interactive fiction less frustrating for players
without nostalgia for it. A scope walk that under-reports serves correctness and
works against that goal.

## The rule: annotate by vocabulary, offer by scope

Two surfaces, and they want different tests. Conflating them is the defect.

**The reveal — words in text the story ALREADY PRINTED.** The test is *"does the
game know this word?"*, answered by the dictionary. No scope walk, no openness
attribute, no inference. Arthur printed "the crystal imbedded in the torque's
knob" on the player's screen; lighting a word already visible reveals nothing
that has not been told, so there is no spoiler to defend against and no reason
for a conservative test. A description that names a sword sitting in another room
lights it too — confirmed as intended.

**The band's noun column — words the player has NOT necessarily seen.** Here we
are proposing rather than annotating, so a boundary is real: the flat dictionary
is hundreds of words including objects from rooms never entered, useless as a
list and genuinely spoiling.

## The inversion found while scoping this

**The engine with the MOST introspection offers the LEAST vocabulary.**

`command_band`'s `None =>` arm — the fallback for an engine with no live object
tree — reads `state.seen_words`, the words the story has printed. The Z-machine
arm uses the object tree instead, and *only* the object tree. So a Glulx story
offers everything the story printed while Zork I and Arthur get scope-only.

That, not depth and not `infer_open_attr`, is why the Arthur crystal is missing
from the band. Having a real object tree made that path look authoritative enough
to use alone.

## The three tiers, two of which already exist

| tier | source | today |
|---|---|---|
| in the room / in hand | `state.scope_words` | live; SQ-1133 widened it to an opened container in hand |
| printed on screen | `state.seen_words` | **slides**, and non-Z-machine only |
| the flat dictionary | `state.dict_words` | Tab completion only, which is correct |

Tab completion already runs all three (`input.rs`, the `suggest` call site). The
band does not.

## The work

1. **`refresh_seen_words` must accumulate.** It currently does
   `state.seen_words = words` over `recent_transcript_text(state)` — a sliding
   window, so a word scrolls out of the list as the transcript moves on.
2. **The Z-machine band reads scope AND seen**, not scope alone.
3. **The reveal annotates by vocabulary**, dropping its scope test entirely.

## Persistence: nothing new is persisted — this is the recipe/result rule

Decided with the user. `seen_words` must be **derived from the whole restored
transcript**, not stored beside it.

The archive already carries the full transcript, and a restore replaces it
wholesale exactly as `*mapper = ac.mapper` replaces the map. So deriving the word
set from it gives, for free:

* accumulation within a session — the transcript grows;
* **per-save semantics on restore** — the transcript is replaced, so restoring to
  before the crystal was read takes the word away again, which is the correct
  answer and was the user's own observation;
* nothing new in the archive.

Adding a `seen_words` field would store a derived result beside the input it comes
from — the exact hazard `ArchiveContents` warns about two fields below `mapper`,
where `paint_ground` must RESET when absent so "a restore must not leave the
pre-restore surface standing".

Implementation consequence: accumulate incrementally per turn over new lines
(tokenise + dictionary lookup is the cost), and rebuild from scratch at the one
place the transcript is replaced — restore.

## Relationship to SQ-1134

**This demotes SQ-1134 rather than replacing it.** With the reveal annotating by
vocabulary and the band reading `seen_words`, `infer_open_attr` stops being what
blocks the user's reported Arthur crystal. SQ-1134 remains a real correctness
question about the scope WALK — an opened container's contents should be in
`scope_words` — but it is no longer urgent and no longer gates a user-visible
defect.

Also related: [[SQ-1107]], whose persistent-highlight redesign is the surface this
rule applies to, and SQ-1133, which built the single `scope_split` spelling that
tier 1 reads.

## Shipped in 921b7a6d, with one departure from the design

Everything settled in the brief is in: the band's noun columns carry the printed
words under the object tree's rows in ONE column, dimmed through a new
`band.item:seen`; the header is the `HereSource` tri-state; `refresh_seen_words`
accumulates newest-first and persists nothing (the invalidation is
`reset_transcript_sidecars`, which every transcript-replacing site already
calls, and the five restore sites rebuild from what they restored); the reveal
drops its scope walk entirely and `RevealTier` with it; `WITH…` gets the same
block; there is no cap.

### The departure: the noun bit could not be the filter

The brief assumed the block's existing filter — "a word the story's dictionary
marks a NOUN and does not write into a grammar line" (SQ-1042) — would carry
over. It cannot. **The dictionary flag byte is unusable on the three Infocom
Version 6 games**, which is exactly the family Arthur is in.

`zvm::grammar::decode_roles` reads `GrammarFormat::InfocomV6` as Inform's layout
— `verb` = $01, `noun` = $80 — and $80 is not where these games keep it.
Measured through the real sessions:

| story | frame | what the noun bit selects |
|---|---|---|
| Arthur r74 | churchyard, 12 taps + `x torque` | `are is was were will` — and NOT `crystal`, `torque`, `sword` |
| Zork Zero r393 | opening, 12 taps + `look` | `a all and of the then` |
| Shogun r322 | opening, 12 taps + `look` | nothing at all |

So the shipped filter would have put "is, was, were" in Arthur's column and
still not the crystal. Arthur's own bytes fit $01 verb / $02 noun / $04
adjective / $40 direction perfectly (`torque` `sword` `knob` `sliver` = $02;
`crystal` `stone` `horse` = $06; `large` `small` = $04; `the` = $20; `and`
`with` `on` = $00), and both Zork Zero and Shogun falsify that reading — Shogun
files `the` as $02, Zork Zero as $A0. **The V6 layout is not one guess away, and
this quest did not guess it.**

### What it does instead: `Introspect::all_object_words`

A new seam: every object in the story with the words the parser files it under.
Exact, family-independent, needs no flag layout, and it is what the code comment
in `refresh_objects` already named as the right question
("a word no object answers to is not a thing"). Default `None`, so Glulx and
Scott keep the role-bit filter unchanged; zvm answers through the `ParseNames`
it already caches. Asked once a TURN — a walk of the object table has no
business running per frame — in `refresh_seen_words`, which now produces two
lists in one pass: `seen_words` (every printed word the dictionary holds, which
is completion's tier and unchanged in meaning) and `seen_nouns` (the things,
which is the band's block).

On the Arthur specimen it yields 48 things out of 104 printed known words, with
`crystal` leading, and `torque` `sword` `stone` `runes` `gravestones` present.
It inherits whatever the OBJECTS say, exactly as the old filter inherited
whatever the flags said — Arthur's `password` object rewrites its own parse
names as its puzzle runs and reads `password passwords word words fair begot
there lot` at boot, so `was`, `by` and `so` leak in. That is pinned in
`the_v6_noun_bit_names_the_wrong_words_and_the_objects_do_not` rather than
hidden.

### Cases

- `command_band::arthurs_crystal_reaches_the_band_once_the_story_has_named_it` —
  the reported defect, on `stories/arthur-r74-s890714.z6` r74/s890714, churchyard,
  12 taps + `n` + `x torque`. Guards that the frame is the churchyard and that
  `crystal` is absent BEFORE the description prints.
- `command_band::the_v6_noun_bit_names_the_wrong_words_and_the_objects_do_not`
- `story_word_scrape::a_word_printed_twenty_turns_ago_is_still_offered`
- `story_word_scrape::restoring_to_before_a_word_was_printed_takes_the_word_back`
- `render::command_band::tests::the_what_header_says_only_what_is_true_of_the_whole_column`
- `render::command_band::tests::a_seen_row_is_dimmed_and_a_scope_row_is_not`
- `word_reveal` rewritten: `a_word_the_story_knows_lights_wherever_the_thing_is`
  now asserts the reverse of what it asserted before, which is the reframe made
  visible.

All four fixes falsified against the original symptom before being trusted.

### Left standing, for whoever reads the V6 flag byte properly

`decode_roles`'s `InfocomV6` arm claims a `noun` bit it does not have. Nothing
user-visible depends on it now, and `vocab::nouns()` (the return probe's control
words) still reads it — on Arthur that is nine words out of 1059, which the
`typeable_name` doc already records. Fixing it would want an authoritative
source, not the pattern above.
